**Chapter 22**

**Closing Thoughts: Choosing Wisely in a World of Good Options**

This book has walked through the history of defensive ammunition testing, explained the science of wound ballistics, surveyed every common defensive caliber, and provided detailed guidance on ammunition selection. If you have read this far, you now possess more knowledge about defensive ammunition than ninety-nine percent of concealed carriers — and possibly more than you strictly need for the decisions you face.

The purpose of this final chapter is not to add more data or introduce new concepts. It is to step back, synthesize what has been covered, and provide practical guidance on how to make ammunition choices that balance competing priorities in ways that work for your specific situation.

**The Zero-Sum Reality: Every Choice Has a Cost**

Ammunition selection is fundamentally a series of trade-offs. You cannot maximize every desirable characteristic simultaneously. Larger calibers deliver wider permanent wound channels but require larger, heavier firearms with lower capacity. Higher velocities improve barrier penetration and expansion reliability but produce sharper recoil and accelerated wear. Lighter firearms are easier to carry all day but harder to shoot accurately and more punishing in felt recoil.

These trade-offs are not marketing limitations or engineering failures that will be solved by future innovation. They are consequences of physics that no amount of research and development can eliminate. When you gain something — more power, less weight, higher capacity — you pay for it somewhere else.

The question is not how to avoid trade-offs. The question is which trade-offs you are willing to accept.

If you prioritize maximum concealability, you accept that your firearm will be harder to shoot well and will hold fewer rounds. If you prioritize maximum capacity, you accept that your firearm will be larger and heavier to carry. If you prioritize maximum terminal performance, you accept sharper recoil and the training investment required to shoot it well.

None of these choices is wrong. They are simply different priority structures that lead to different conclusions.

**The Four Non-Negotiable Rules**

Regardless of which caliber you choose, which specific load you select, or which platform you carry, four principles apply universally.

**Rule One: Carry Your Firearm**

The most effective defensive ammunition in the world is useless if the gun it is loaded in is locked in your safe at home. The firearm you carry every day — even if it is a .380 pocket pistol that gun forum experts dismiss — is infinitely more useful than the ideal setup that is too heavy, too bulky, or too uncomfortable to actually carry.

Choose a platform and caliber combination that you will carry consistently, not one that maximizes theoretical performance at the cost of practical carry. A firearm you leave at home because it is inconvenient delivers zero defensive capability when you need it.

**Rule Two: Use What Works in Your Specific Firearm**

Ammunition that produces spectacular gelatin test results but jams in your pistol is worse than mediocre ammunition that functions flawlessly. Reliability is not negotiable.

Test your chosen defensive load in your specific firearm. Fire at least fifty to one hundred rounds without malfunction before trusting it for defensive carry. Verify that it feeds, fires, and ejects reliably. Verify that it shoots to your sights — or close enough that you can adjust your hold without conscious thought under stress.

If a particular load does not work in your gun, choose something else. There are dozens of quality defensive loads available for every common caliber. You do not need the theoretically optimal load. You need a load that works.

**Rule Three: Use Ammunition You Can Practice With**

You cannot become proficient with ammunition you never fire. If your defensive load produces recoil so punishing that you avoid practice, or if it costs so much that you ration your training, you will not develop the skills necessary to use it effectively when it matters.

Choose ammunition that permits you to practice realistically and frequently. For some shooters, this means selecting a milder-recoiling caliber. For others, it means using reduced-recoil practice loads that approximate the point of impact and felt recoil of their carry ammunition without the cost or punishment of firing premium defensive rounds extensively.

The goal is not to shoot your carry ammunition exclusively in practice — though you should fire it periodically to verify continued reliability and refresh your familiarity with its characteristics. The goal is to develop skills with ammunition similar enough to your carry load that those skills transfer when you load your defensive rounds.

**Rule Four: Carry Factory Ammunition, Not Handloads**

This is one of the few areas where a categorical prohibition is appropriate: do not carry handloaded ammunition for defensive purposes.

The legal risks are real. Prosecutors have argued that handloaded ammunition demonstrates premeditation or bloodthirstiness — that you were so eager to maximize lethality that you custom-engineered your ammunition specifically to kill. These arguments are legally and technically absurd, but they have been made, and they introduce complications that factory ammunition does not.

Factory ammunition from reputable manufacturers is used by law enforcement agencies nationwide. It has been tested, documented, and adopted by professionals. You can point to published data showing that your chosen load meets FBI standards. You can demonstrate that police departments trust the same ammunition you carry. These are powerful rebuttals to claims that you chose unusually lethal or reckless ammunition.

Handloads offer none of these advantages. They introduce legal risk with no corresponding defensive benefit. Save your handloads for practice and competition. Carry factory ammunition for defense.

> ***Key Takeaway: The four non-negotiable rules are: carry your firearm consistently, use ammunition that works reliably in your specific gun, practice enough to develop genuine proficiency, and carry factory ammunition instead of handloads. Everything else is preference and priority balancing. Get these four right, and your ammunition choices will serve you well regardless of which specific caliber or load you select.***

**The Convergence of Modern Ammunition Quality**

One of the most important developments in defensive ammunition over the past two decades is the convergence of quality across major manufacturers. Twenty years ago, the performance gap between the best and worst defensive hollow-points was dramatic. Some loads expanded reliably; others did not. Some penetrated adequately; others fragmented or stopped shallow.

That era is over. Today, virtually every defensive load from a major manufacturer delivers FBI-compliant terminal performance. Expansion is reliable, penetration is adequate, weight retention is high, and barrier performance is good. The gap between the "best" and "tenth-best" nine-millimeter hollow-point is measured in fractions of an inch of penetration and hundredths of an inch of expansion — differences that laboratory instruments can measure but that real-world defensive outcomes will not reveal.

This convergence has practical implications. It means you cannot make a seriously wrong choice if you select any quality defensive load from a reputable manufacturer. It also means that obsessive comparison-shopping and endless forum debates about fractional performance differences are largely wasted effort.

The meaningful variables are not which brand of bonded-core hollow-point you select from among the top-tier options. They are whether your chosen load feeds reliably, whether you can shoot it accurately, and whether you have trained enough to deliver effective hits under stress. Those factors determine outcomes. The difference between one premium hollow-point and another does not.

**Making Your Choice: A Practical Process**

Start with the platform. Which firearm will you actually carry? Not the one you wish you could carry comfortably, or the one that delivers maximum theoretical performance — the one that fits your actual lifestyle, clothing, and comfort requirements.

From that platform, your caliber is largely determined. If you carry a subcompact 9mm, your caliber is nine-millimeter. If you carry a .38 Special revolver, your caliber is .38 Special. The caliber debate is settled by your platform choice.

Within that caliber, select two or three highly-rated defensive loads from major manufacturers. Ignore boutique brands with limited track records. Choose from manufacturers that supply law enforcement and publish test data.

Test those loads in your specific firearm for reliability and accuracy. Fire fifty rounds of each without malfunction. Shoot groups at defensive distances and verify that they hit to your sights. If one load clearly outperforms the others in reliability or accuracy, choose it. If they all perform similarly — which is likely — choose based on availability and cost.

Document your choice. Save targets. Save packaging. Note the date you tested and selected the load. Seal this documentation and store it. You may never need it, but if the question arises of why you used what you used, you have a solid answer: you tested quality ammunition, selected the one that worked best in your gun, and made an informed decision based on established performance standards.

Then train. The ammunition you chose matters far less than your ability to deliver accurate, rapid fire under stress.

**Final Thoughts**

The caliber wars will continue as long as people carry firearms. Arguments will rage over nine-millimeter versus .45 ACP, over whether +P ammunition is necessary, over which specific hollow-point design is "the best." These debates are largely sound and fury signifying very little.

The truth is that modern defensive ammunition works. Nine-millimeter works. .40 S&W works. .45 ACP works. .38 Special with quality loads works. .380 ACP with appropriate ammunition and realistic expectations works. They all work, provided you choose quality loads, verify reliability in your firearm, and train to shoot them well.

Your ammunition choice matters less than your ability to recognize threats early, your legal knowledge of when force is justified, your skill at delivering accurate fire under stress, and your willingness to practice consistently. Focus your energy on the variables that determine outcomes — training, awareness, legal knowledge, shot placement — not on chasing fractional performance differences between already-excellent ammunition options.

Choose wisely. Train diligently. Carry responsibly. And recognize that the best defensive ammunition is the ammunition you can shoot well, that works in your gun, and that you will actually carry.
