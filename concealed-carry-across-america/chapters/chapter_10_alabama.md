**Chapter 10**

**Alabama**

*[IMAGE: Alabama state outline with a firearm-friendliness rating bar showing approximately 85/100. State flag accent and "Heart of Dixie" tagline. Clean infographic header.]*

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — One of the most permissive states in the country for armed travelers.*

Alabama has earned its reputation as a deeply gun-friendly state. With the adoption of permitless carry, the state formalized what had long been a cultural reality — that firearms ownership and carry are woven into the fabric of daily life across the state.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Full preemption in effect; local governments face liability for non-compliance
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public locations with some exceptions
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued by county sheriffs to residents; constitutional carry also in effect
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 19 and older who may lawfully possess a handgun may carry openly or concealed
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid out-of-state permits honored for non-residents
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried concealed anywhere in a vehicle; loaded rifles and shotguns are generally permitted except in specific restricted zones
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to institutional policy as defined by statute
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Subject to policy established by local school boards
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Parking lot owners may set their own firearms policies for visitors
- **State parks:** Concealed carry permitted for recognized license holders
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Armed individuals may carry while dining in the food-service area
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted for license holders unless the institution has posted a prohibition; individuals without permits need the institution's explicit consent
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy; no force-of-law posting provisions
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Although permitless carry is now the law, the state continues to issue carry permits through county sheriffs. These licenses are available to qualified residents aged 19 and older, with terms ranging from one to five years. A lifetime upgrade option is also available.

While a permit is no longer required for everyday carry within the state, obtaining one provides meaningful advantages — particularly for out-of-state travel, where reciprocity agreements often depend on holding a physical license. Permit holders also enjoy expanded privileges at certain locations within Alabama itself. Licensed carriers may bring firearms to most athletic events and sporting venues, provided the facility does not employ active security screening devices such as metal detectors or magnetometers. Resident permit holders are also generally permitted to carry on K-12 school property unless the local school board has adopted a policy prohibiting it.

Restrictions still apply. Firearms are prohibited in universally restricted areas, certain government buildings, and locations specifically designated as off-limits under state law, regardless of permit status.

**Without a Permit**

Any individual aged 19 or older who is legally eligible to possess a handgun may carry it — openly or concealed — throughout most public areas in the state. State law explicitly protects non-threatening firearm carry from being charged as disorderly conduct, recognizing that the mere presence of a lawfully carried weapon does not constitute a disturbance.

Vehicle carry is broad. Handguns may be stored virtually anywhere in a vehicle, including the glove compartment, center console, door pocket, or under a seat. Loaded long guns — rifles and shotguns — may also be transported in vehicles, though specific areas such as state parks, wildlife management areas, and state land division properties require long guns to be unloaded while inside a vehicle.

Open carry of a loaded handgun on foot is legal in most public spaces. However, carrying on private property — whether openly or concealed — requires either a valid carry permit or the property owner's express permission.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Certain locations are off-limits to all carriers regardless of licensing status. These include buildings with controlled-access entry points such as turnstiles or metal detectors, law enforcement facilities, hospitals, courthouses, and the offices of elected officials. These restricted buildings must display signage indicating the prohibition, and they may not prevent individuals from keeping firearms locked and secured inside a closed vehicle compartment on the premises.

Athletic events carry a nuanced restriction: non-permitted individuals may not carry at all, while permitted carriers are allowed unless the venue employs active security screening measures. Carrying a firearm at or near a public demonstration is prohibited for all persons.

During any encounter with law enforcement, individuals must disclose that they are in possession of a firearm if directly asked. During a traffic stop specifically, no person may touch or knowingly handle a handgun under any circumstances.

> ***Key Takeaway: Alabama is one of the nation's most accommodating states for armed travelers. Constitutional carry allows anyone 19 and older to carry without a license, all out-of-state permits are honored, and vehicle carry is virtually unrestricted. However, certain buildings, athletic events, and demonstrations remain off-limits — and any encounter with law enforcement requires honest disclosure when asked. Obtaining a permit remains advisable for the reciprocity benefits it provides during interstate travel.***
