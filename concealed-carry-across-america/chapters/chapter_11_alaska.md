**Chapter 11**

**Alaska**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 95/100 — Among the most permissive jurisdictions in the nation for firearm owners and travelers.*

In a state where the wilderness is never far and self-reliance is a way of life, firearms are not a political statement — they are a practical necessity. The overwhelming majority of the state's residents own and regularly use firearms, and the legal framework reflects that reality. Alaska was one of the earliest adopters of permitless concealed carry, and its broad protections for armed citizens make it one of the most welcoming destinations for traveling gun owners.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Statewide preemption statute in effect; regulations are largely consistent across all jurisdictions
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful throughout most public areas and widely accepted by the community
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; five-year validity
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any person 21 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry concealed without a license
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every other state and locality are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Individuals 21 and older may carry loaded, concealed firearms anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for all gun owners, whether permitted or not
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** State permit holders must unload and encase the firearm
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Immediately upon any official contact
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Any non-felon may carry a firearm for personal defense
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Concealed handgun carry permitted in dining areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy; force-of-law posting provisions may apply
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

The state issues carry licenses to qualified residents, valid for five years. These permits authorize the holder to carry a loaded, concealed handgun in most public locations throughout the state. Every valid carry permit from every other U.S. state and political subdivision is recognized, provided the holder is at least 21 years of age and legally eligible to possess firearms.

While the state adopted permitless carry in 2003 — making it one of the first to follow Vermont's model of license-free concealed carry — many residents still choose to obtain a formal permit. The primary motivation is reciprocity: a state-issued permit unlocks carry privileges in dozens of other states that require a physical license as a condition of recognition. For anyone who travels outside state borders with any regularity, maintaining an active permit is a practical decision.

**Without a Permit**

Any individual aged 21 or older who is legally entitled to possess a firearm may carry a concealed, loaded handgun on their person or inside their vehicle throughout most public areas. This includes handguns worn under outerwear, stored in bags or briefcases being carried by the individual, or placed in vehicle compartments such as the glove box, center console, or door pocket.

Loaded rifles and shotguns may be carried openly while on foot or transported in a vehicle using gun racks, soft cases, or similar methods. There are no special restrictions on long gun transport beyond the standard prohibitions that apply in designated restricted areas.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited in bars, K-12 school buildings, day care centers, and assisted living facilities. Carrying inside a private residence without the express permission of the property owner is also unlawful. Licensed carriers may bring concealed handguns into restaurants, provided they refrain from consuming any alcohol while on the premises.

Any person carrying a concealed firearm must immediately inform a law enforcement officer during any official contact that they are armed. This duty-to-notify requirement is proactive — it applies before the officer asks, not merely in response to a question.

The state maintains strong preemption protections that prevent local governments from enacting their own regulations on firearm carry, transport, ownership, or possession. Additionally, private businesses and most state or municipal properties may not prohibit firearms stored inside privately owned vehicles parked on their premises.

> ***Key Takeaway: Alaska's combination of permitless carry, universal permit recognition, strong preemption, and vehicle-protection laws makes it one of the most accommodating states in the country for armed travelers. The only significant caution is the immediate duty-to-notify requirement during law enforcement encounters — failure to disclose that you are armed before being asked is a separate offense. Travelers visiting from other states should feel confident carrying here, but should still obtain a home-state permit for the reciprocity benefits it provides elsewhere.***
