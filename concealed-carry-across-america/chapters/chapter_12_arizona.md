**Chapter 12**

**Arizona**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 95/100 — A top-tier state for firearms freedom, with constitutional carry and robust legal protections.*

Few states have embraced firearms rights as comprehensively as Arizona. Open carry is a visible and unremarkable part of daily life in cities and towns across the state, and the adoption of permitless concealed carry extended that freedom to those who prefer discretion. Combined with strong preemption laws and broad vehicle protections, the Grand Canyon State consistently ranks among the most firearm-friendly jurisdictions in the country.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law with civil liability for non-compliant local governments
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful throughout most public areas and widely practiced
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and qualified U.S. citizens; five-year validity
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 21 and older who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry concealed without a license
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every other state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful deadly weapon (broader than handguns only)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms — concealed or openly carried — permitted for any law-abiding individual 21 or older
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for all gun owners in vehicles on campus
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permitted for state license holders with an unloaded handgun
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed carry in outdoor areas permitted for individuals 21 and older; buildings may impose restrictions
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for license holders dining in food-service areas; prohibited for non-permit holders
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy; force-of-law posting provisions may apply
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Despite the availability of permitless carry, the state continues to issue concealed weapons permits to qualified residents and U.S. citizens. These five-year licenses are recognized by every other state, and the state reciprocates by honoring permits from all other jurisdictions — provided the holder is legally present, physically carrying the permit, at least 21 years old, and has no outstanding warrants or indictments.

Holding a permit unlocks a significant practical advantage within the state: permit holders may carry in restaurants and other establishments that serve alcohol for on-premises consumption, provided the venue has not posted a prohibition. Non-permitted individuals are barred from carrying in these locations entirely, regardless of signage. For travelers who plan to dine out, this distinction alone can justify obtaining a permit.

Additionally, many travelers from other states find that an Arizona-issued permit — which is available to non-residents — provides excellent reciprocity coverage for interstate travel, making it a popular choice as a secondary or supplemental license.

**Without a Permit**

Any law-abiding person aged 21 or older may carry a concealed or openly displayed firearm in most public locations and inside any vehicle. During a law enforcement encounter, individuals must disclose that they are armed only if directly asked.

Persons between 18 and 20 years of age face tighter restrictions. While on foot, they may only carry openly — concealed carry is not authorized for this age group. Inside a vehicle, their firearm must be stored in a holster, the glove compartment, or the trunk. Concealed carry on the person while in a vehicle is not permitted for those under 21.

One important limitation: concealed carry by non-permitted individuals is prohibited inside any business that serves alcohol for on-premises consumption. This restriction applies regardless of whether signs are posted. Only holders of a valid concealed weapons permit may carry in these venues.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

The state enforces strong preemption laws that prevent local municipalities from creating their own firearms regulations, and local governments face civil penalties for attempting to do so. Despite this broad protection, several categories of locations remain off-limits statewide.

Firearms are prohibited in K-12 schools, at polling places during elections, and at nuclear or hydroelectric power generation facilities. Certain public colleges and universities may restrict carry if the prohibition has been established by the Board of Regents.

A nuanced set of restrictions applies to what the state defines as "public establishments" — such as government-owned or government-leased buildings, sports arenas, and similar venues. These locations must post signage indicating the firearms prohibition and are required to provide secure storage facilities where visitors can check their weapons for the duration of their stay. "Public events" — officially licensed gatherings such as outdoor concerts or festivals authorized by a government body — may also prohibit firearms, but informal or unlicensed gatherings do not trigger this restriction.

Vehicle-based firearm rights receive strong statutory protection. Property owners may not prohibit firearms kept inside privately owned vehicles parked on their premises. Schools and universities are likewise barred from enforcing policies that prohibit lawful firearm possession in vehicles on public rights-of-way adjacent to campus.

> ***Key Takeaway: Arizona offers near-total freedom for armed citizens and travelers. Constitutional carry, universal permit recognition, vehicle protections, and a culture of open carry make it one of the easiest states to navigate while armed. The key distinction to remember is the restaurant carry rule: only permit holders may carry in establishments that serve alcohol. Non-permitted individuals are prohibited regardless of signage. For that reason alone — plus the reciprocity benefits for interstate travel — obtaining a permit remains a smart decision even in a permitless carry state.***
