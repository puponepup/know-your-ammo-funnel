**Chapter 13**

**Arkansas**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — A strongly pro-gun state with permitless carry and deep-rooted firearms culture.*

Rooted in a frontier heritage that stretches back to the mid-1800s, Arkansas maintains a firm commitment to individual firearms rights. The state adopted constitutional carry provisions and honors all out-of-state permits, making it a straightforward destination for armed travelers. With one of the lowest age thresholds for permitless carry in the nation — 18 years old — the state extends firearm freedoms broadly to its residents and visitors alike.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Statewide preemption statute in effect; laws are largely consistent
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in public areas where firearms are not specifically prohibited
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; enhanced permits also available
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any person 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm and is on a journey may carry concealed
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every other state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Recent court decisions support loaded handguns anywhere in a vehicle; long guns may be transported in plain view or in commercial gun cases
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited if the institution has posted a restriction; otherwise lawful for permit holders
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permitted for state license holders with a loaded handgun, provided the parking lot is publicly owned; private schools set their own rules
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may set their own firearms policies for visitors' vehicles
- **State parks:** Recognized license holders may carry in outdoor areas; government buildings within parks remain restricted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for license holders dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the institution's leadership has provided explicit notice of consent
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy; force-of-law posting provisions may apply
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

The state issues concealed carry permits to qualified residents, with an enhanced permit option that provides expanded privileges in certain restricted areas. Standard permits authorize the holder to carry a loaded, concealed handgun in most public locations throughout the state. Enhanced permits — which require additional training — may allow carry in locations that are otherwise restricted for standard permit holders.

All valid permits from every other U.S. state are honored. The state also issues permits specifically to military members stationed within its borders and their spouses, though some other states may limit their recognition to resident-only permits.

Permit holders enjoy several advantages over non-permitted carriers, including the ability to carry in restaurants that serve alcohol and in state parks. At K-12 schools, permit holders may keep a loaded handgun in their vehicle when parked in a publicly owned lot. At colleges and universities, carry is generally lawful for permit holders unless the institution has specifically posted a prohibition.

**Without a Permit**

The state's permitless carry provision is notable for its low age threshold: any person aged 18 or older who can legally possess a firearm and is engaged in a journey — defined broadly as any travel outside the person's home or immediate property — may carry a concealed handgun without obtaining a license.

Inside a vehicle, recent judicial decisions have affirmed the right to carry a loaded handgun in any position within the passenger compartment. Long guns may be transported in plain view or stored in purpose-built gun cases.

Individuals carrying without a permit are not authorized to carry in restaurants that serve alcohol, at colleges that have posted restrictions, or in certain other locations where only licensed carriers are permitted.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Despite broad carry freedoms, several categories of locations remain off-limits regardless of permit status. These include K-12 school buildings, government offices, courthouses, and other facilities designated by state law. Carrying at a polling place during an election or inside a facility that primarily serves alcohol is prohibited for all persons.

Houses of worship present a specific restriction: carry is prohibited by default unless the institution's leadership has formally provided notice that firearms are welcome. This is a "permission required" model rather than a "posted prohibition" model — meaning silence from the institution equals a prohibition, not an invitation.

The state maintains a preemption statute that prevents local governments from enacting conflicting firearms regulations, though the protections are somewhat narrower than those in the most permissive neighboring states. Travelers should be aware that certain locations — particularly private property and some municipal facilities — may impose restrictions that carry legal consequences.

> ***Key Takeaway: Arkansas is a welcoming state for armed travelers, with permitless carry starting at age 18, universal permit recognition, and favorable vehicle carry laws supported by recent court decisions. The primary nuances to watch are the "journey" requirement for permitless carry (which is broadly interpreted but technically applies to travel outside the home), the house-of-worship prohibition that defaults to "no carry" without explicit permission, and the distinction between standard and enhanced permits for expanded location access. Obtaining a permit — especially the enhanced version — provides meaningful additional privileges.***
