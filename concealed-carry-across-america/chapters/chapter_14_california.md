**Chapter 14**

**California**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 15/100 — One of the most restrictive states in the nation. Extreme caution required for all armed travelers.*

California represents one of the most challenging legal environments for gun owners in the United States. The state does not recognize any out-of-state concealed carry permits, prohibits open carry of both loaded and unloaded firearms in virtually all public locations, mandates gun registration for new residents, restricts magazine capacity, bans entire categories of semi-automatic firearms, and regulates ammunition sales. Recent legislative sessions have introduced additional restrictions including mandatory safe storage requirements for all unattended firearms. Court challenges are ongoing and have temporarily suspended enforcement of some location-based restrictions, but the overall framework remains deeply hostile to armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Registration required for all firearms brought into the state by new residents; ammunition sales regulated; "safe storage" of unattended firearms mandated after January 1, 2026
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** So-called "assault weapons" prohibited; magazines holding more than 10 rounds prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Limited preemption — local governments may impose additional restrictions on carry
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; self-defense standards governed by jury instructions rather than statute
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas — both loaded and unloaded
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued by county sheriffs to residents only; two-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handguns must be completely unloaded and secured in a locked trunk, locked utility box, or locked gun case; they may not be stored in glove compartments or center consoles; long guns must be unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Firearm possession prohibited on all campus property
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearms must be unloaded and in locked containers
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Most carry by permit holders is prohibited
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** All carry prohibited, even for permit holders
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the institution has posted signage explicitly allowing it (enforcement currently suspended by court order)
- **Hotels:** Statutory provision allows loaded firearms, but possession may be restricted through "consent of owner" rules
- **Airports:** All carry prohibited for permit holders in terminal areas
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in outdoor areas and vehicles; buildings may be restricted

**For California Permit Holders**

Obtaining a carry permit in California requires applying through the county sheriff. Applicants must be at least 21, pass comprehensive background checks including mental health evaluations, and complete certified firearms training. Permits are valid for two years and authorize concealed carry of handguns only.

Even with a valid permit, the list of prohibited locations is extensive. Permit holders are generally barred from carrying in day care centers, government buildings, parks (local and state), bars, restaurants serving alcohol, playgrounds, athletic venues, colleges, K-12 schools, casinos, stadiums, libraries, zoos, museums, airports, amusement parks, polling places, wildlife refuges, gun shows, police stations, banks, hospitals, public transit facilities, houses of worship, and public gatherings conducted under government permit. Additionally, private businesses are presumed to prohibit firearms unless they have posted signage explicitly allowing them — an "informed consent" framework that reverses the common rule in most other states.

Court rulings have temporarily suspended enforcement of many of these location-based restrictions pending further review, but the legal landscape remains fluid. Permit holders may store a handgun in a locked container within their vehicle while visiting restricted locations.

**Without a California Permit**

Travelers without a California-issued permit face severe restrictions. Handguns must be completely unloaded, stored inside a locked trunk or a permanently mounted locked utility box, or secured in a locked gun case if transported in the passenger compartment. Glove compartments and center consoles are never acceptable storage locations, regardless of whether they lock.

Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded during transport but are not required to be in locked containers. A firearm is considered "loaded" if ammunition is present in the chamber or in any magazine attached to the weapon. Because the state interprets "attached" broadly, storing ammunition in a separate container from the firearm is the safest practice.

Open carry on foot — whether loaded or unloaded — is prohibited in all public places. Exceptions exist for licensed hunters actively engaged in lawful hunting or shooting activities and for firearm possession at established campsites or in RVs classified as temporary residences.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

The state bans possession and importation of magazines holding more than 10 rounds. Semi-automatic firearms classified as "assault weapons" under state definitions, .50 caliber center-fire rifles, and certain other categories are prohibited. Ammunition sales are regulated and subject to background checks.

While the state has a preemption statute that prevents local governments from regulating licensing and registration, local jurisdictions retain broad authority to impose additional restrictions on carry. Some municipalities — including Denver-adjacent mountain communities and Bay Area cities — have enacted ordinances that significantly exceed state-level restrictions.

New residents are required to register all firearms they bring into the state. Beginning in 2026, all firearms must be stored in a "safe" manner when not attended, adding yet another layer of compliance requirements.

> ***Key Takeaway: California is a hostile environment for armed travelers and should be treated accordingly. The state recognizes no out-of-state permits, prohibits open carry entirely, restricts vehicle transport to unloaded firearms in locked containers, bans standard-capacity magazines, and imposes location-based restrictions on permit holders that effectively cover most public spaces. If your route requires passing through California, follow strict McClure-Volkmer transport protocols: firearms unloaded, locked in a hard-sided case, stored in the trunk, with ammunition stored separately. Do not stop longer than necessary for fuel or emergencies. If California is your destination, consult a California-licensed attorney before traveling with any firearm.***
