**Chapter 15**

**Colorado**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 45/100 — A state in transition, trending toward greater restriction. Vehicle carry remains relatively permissive, but on-foot carry and local regulations present increasing challenges.*

Colorado has undergone a significant political and demographic shift in recent years, and its firearms laws reflect that transformation. Once considered reliably gun-friendly, the state has enacted a series of restrictions — including a magazine capacity limit, expanded "gun-free" zones, and new vehicle storage requirements — that would have been unlikely just a decade ago. Despite these changes, vehicle carry remains relatively permissive, and the state still issues concealed carry permits and recognizes permits from many other states under conditional reciprocity.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Magazines exceeding 15 rounds are prohibited if acquired after July 1, 2013; some municipalities (including Denver) impose additional restrictions on semi-automatic firearms
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Weakened preemption — local governments may regulate on-foot carry; vehicle carry is protected statewide
- **Self-defense rights:** Castle Doctrine codified for the home; stand-your-ground protections in public are not codified in statute
- **Open carry:** Lawful under state law, but local municipalities may independently prohibit or restrict it
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued by county sheriffs to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — only permits from states that reciprocally honor Colorado permits are recognized; holder must be a resident of the issuing state, at least 21, and in possession of a matching state-issued ID
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle for self-defense; rifles and shotguns must be chamber-unloaded during vehicle transport
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for permit holders; handguns only
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permitted for Colorado permit holders with a loaded handgun
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed carry of handguns by recognized license holders is permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for license holders dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Denver International prohibits most on-foot carry; non-secure areas of other airports generally allow carry
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

The state issues concealed carry permits to qualified residents aged 21 and older through county sheriffs. Permits are valid for five years. Non-resident permits are not issued, but the state recognizes permits from other states on a conditional basis: the issuing state must also honor Colorado permits, the holder must be a resident of the issuing state with a matching driver's license or state ID, and the holder must be at least 21.

Permit holders may carry a concealed handgun in most public locations, including state parks, restaurants that serve alcohol, and houses of worship that have not posted a prohibition. Permit holders may also keep a loaded handgun in their vehicle while parked at K-12 schools and college campuses.

**Without a Permit**

Colorado allows anyone who may lawfully possess a firearm to carry a loaded handgun anywhere inside their vehicle for self-defense purposes. This is one of the state's more permissive provisions and applies regardless of permit status. Long guns transported in vehicles must be chamber-unloaded. In snowmobiles, long guns must also be stored in a case.

Any firearm left in an unattended vehicle must be secured in a locked hard-sided case — or in a locked glove compartment — and the vehicle itself must also be locked. This vehicle storage requirement is relatively new and represents one of several restrictions enacted in recent legislative sessions.

On foot without a permit, concealed carry is prohibited except when actively engaged in lawful hunting. Open carry is legal under state law, but this right has been significantly eroded by the state's weakened preemption statute. Several cities — including Denver and Boulder — now prohibit on-foot carry in numerous public spaces, often enforced through posted signage. Within Denver specifically, all personal carry on foot is restricted to recognized permit holders, and all firearms in vehicles must be concealed from view.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited at polling and ballot-counting facilities, courthouses, local government offices, public buildings with security screening, state legislative buildings, child care centers, K-12 schools (with exceptions for permit holders' vehicles), and colleges.

The magazine ban prohibits magazines capable of holding more than 15 rounds that were not lawfully possessed before July 1, 2013. Pre-existing magazines are grandfathered and remain legal. Some municipalities — particularly Denver and Boulder — have enacted additional restrictions on semi-automatic firearms by local residents, though non-residents passing through in private vehicles are generally exempt from local ordinances.

> ***Key Takeaway: Colorado remains a workable state for armed travelers, primarily because vehicle carry of loaded handguns is broadly permitted regardless of permit status. However, the state is trending restrictive, and on-foot carry has become significantly more complicated due to weak preemption and expanding local "gun-free" zones. Travelers must pay particular attention to Denver's special restrictions. Reciprocity is conditional — verify that your permit qualifies before relying on it. The 15-round magazine limit applies to any magazine acquired after 2013, so travelers should leave higher-capacity magazines at home.***
