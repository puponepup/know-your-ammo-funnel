**Chapter 16**

**Connecticut**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 20/100 — Highly restrictive. No out-of-state permit recognition, strict transport rules, and limited preemption create a difficult environment for armed travelers.*

Connecticut is among the most restrictive states in the Northeast for firearms. The state does not recognize any out-of-state concealed carry permits, prohibits open carry entirely, requires permits or certificates for all transfers of firearms and ammunition, bans magazines holding more than 10 rounds, and restricts importation of military-pattern semi-automatic firearms. Without a valid Connecticut-issued permit, even transporting a handgun through the state requires strict compliance with federal safe-passage provisions. Local governments are free to enact additional restrictions due to the absence of a meaningful preemption statute.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** A permit or certificate is required for all transfers of firearms and ammunition
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Importation of any magazine holding more than 10 rounds or any military-pattern "assault weapon" is prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine gun importation prohibited; bump stocks prohibited; suppressors lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** No preemption statute — local governments may enact and enforce their own firearms ordinances
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas for everyone, including state permit holders
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to both residents and non-residents through the state police; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handgun carry in vehicles is prohibited without a Connecticut permit; limited exceptions for direct transport from point of purchase; long guns must be unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to individual college policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited entirely
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Firearms carry prohibited
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for state permit holders dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy; force-of-law posting may apply (potential felony offense)
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities, but local postings may apply
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted for state permit holders in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Connecticut issues concealed carry permits to both residents and non-residents. Residents must first obtain a temporary permit through their local police department before applying for the full five-year permit through the state police. Non-resident U.S. citizens who hold a carry license from another state may apply directly to the Connecticut State Police.

Permit holders are authorized for concealed carry only — open carry is prohibited for everyone. Several locations remain off-limits even with a valid permit, including K-12 schools, state parks and forests, and the State Capitol complex. Additionally, because Connecticut lacks a statewide preemption statute, local governments can and do impose their own carry restrictions. Travelers should watch for posted signs and research local ordinances before carrying in any municipality.

When leaving a vehicle unattended, permit holders must store their handgun in a locked compartment within the vehicle.

**Without a Connecticut Permit**

The environment for non-permitted travelers is extremely restrictive. Carrying a handgun in a vehicle is prohibited without a valid Connecticut permit. The only narrow exception permits transporting an unloaded handgun in a locked container directly from the point of purchase to the owner's home — a provision that offers no practical value to travelers.

Long guns must be unloaded during vehicle transport. Grandfathered semi-automatic firearms classified as "assault weapons" must additionally be stored in a locked case or the vehicle trunk, with a registration certificate available.

Courts interpret firearm transportation laws strictly. Non-resident travelers should possess only firearms that are completely unloaded and securely cased in compliance with the federal McClure-Volkmer Act or the state's parallel safe-passage statute. Any deviation — including an unplanned stop beyond fuel or emergency — may be interpreted as an illegal possession.

A limited exception exists for non-residents with a carry permit from their home state: they may lawfully transport an unloaded handgun in a locked case or trunk when traveling to an officially recognized shooting competition or collectors exhibition. Carrying proof of participation — such as a registration confirmation or event flier — is strongly recommended.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Connecticut prohibits the importation of magazines holding more than 10 rounds, military-pattern semi-automatic firearms, machine guns, and bump stocks. All transfers of firearms and ammunition require a valid state-issued permit or certificate.

Without preemption protections, local municipalities are free to enact additional restrictions. Many cities prohibit carrying in public parks and government buildings through local ordinances. Travelers who are not intimately familiar with the specific municipality they are visiting should exercise extreme caution and default to the most restrictive interpretation.

> ***Key Takeaway: Connecticut is one of the most challenging states for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is banned outright, vehicle carry requires a state-issued permit, and local governments can impose their own additional restrictions. If you must transport firearms through Connecticut, strict McClure-Volkmer compliance is essential: firearms unloaded, locked in a hard-sided case, stored separately from ammunition, and kept in the trunk or least accessible area of the vehicle. Do not deviate from your route. If Connecticut is your destination and you intend to carry, you must apply for a Connecticut non-resident permit before your trip.***
