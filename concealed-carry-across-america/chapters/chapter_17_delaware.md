**Chapter 17**

**Delaware**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 35/100 — Increasingly restrictive, with recent bans on semi-automatic firearms and high-capacity magazines. Some carry provisions remain more workable than neighboring states.*

Delaware's firearms landscape has shifted meaningfully in recent years. The state enacted bans on military-pattern semi-automatic firearms and magazines holding more than 17 rounds in 2022, and a statewide ban on firearm carry at colleges and universities took effect in 2024. Despite these tightening restrictions, the state retains some provisions that are more accommodating than those of its northeastern neighbors — including open carry on foot, vehicle carry of loaded handguns, and conditional reciprocity with a number of other states.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** A permit is required to purchase a handgun
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Military-pattern semi-automatics prohibited from entering the state; magazines exceeding 17 rounds are prohibited unless the possessor holds a Delaware carry license
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership of machine guns prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** State preemption law in effect, but pre-1985 local ordinances are grandfathered; local governments may regulate open carry in government buildings
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Lawful under state law in a visible belt holster, but local ordinances may impose additional restrictions
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states that reciprocally recognize Delaware licenses and meet similar standards of issuance
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful deadly weapon (broader than handguns only)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** A loaded handgun may be carried openly (in plain view) or stored in the trunk; long guns must be unloaded in vehicles
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Permit holders may keep a loaded handgun secured in their vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permitted for Delaware license holders with a loaded handgun
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Delaware permit holders may carry anywhere in the park; out-of-state permit holders and non-permittees face additional restrictions, particularly in buildings and campgrounds
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for license holders dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities; check for local postings
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Delaware issues concealed carry licenses to qualified residents only. These licenses authorize the concealed carry of any lawful deadly weapon — a provision that is broader than many states, which limit carry to handguns.

The state recognizes permits from other states on a conditional basis: the issuing state must honor Delaware licenses, must provide a similar degree of protection as Delaware's own licensing process, and must not demonstrate a pattern of issuing permits to convicted felons. Some states issue multiple tiers of permits, and only those meeting these standards will be recognized. Travelers should verify that their specific permit type qualifies before relying on reciprocity.

Delaware permit holders enjoy broad carry privileges, including access to most areas within state parks and forests. Out-of-state permit holders with recognized permits may also carry but face additional restrictions in park buildings and campgrounds.

**Without a Permit**

Delaware allows the transport of a loaded handgun in a vehicle under specific conditions: the firearm must be either openly visible (such as on the seat or dashboard) or stored in the trunk or another area that is not readily accessible. Loaded handguns may not be carried in the glove compartment, center console, or concealed on the person without a valid carry license.

On foot, open carry is lawful — the handgun must be clearly visible in a belt holster. However, carrying a concealed weapon without a license is classified as a felony. Non-licensed travelers who choose to open carry should proceed with caution, as the legal distinction between open and concealed can be interpreted strictly.

Long guns must be unloaded whenever they are transported in a vehicle, regardless of permit status. Any firearm left in an unattended vehicle must be stored in a locked container, trunk, or glove compartment.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Delaware prohibits the importation and possession of military-pattern semi-automatic firearms and magazines exceeding 17 rounds. A limited exception exists for Delaware residents who obtained carry licenses before the ban took effect (they may continue to possess high-capacity magazines) and for individuals who owned banned firearms before the restrictions were enacted. Out-of-state travelers should not bring restricted items into the state under any circumstances.

Firearms are prohibited in courthouses, police stations, K-12 schools, colleges and universities (though permit holders may keep firearms secured in their vehicles), and at polling places during elections — provided the location is posted.

The state's preemption law broadly limits local regulation of firearms, but ordinances enacted before 1985 are grandfathered. Local governments also retain the authority to restrict firearms within their own government buildings. The practical effect is that some municipalities may have legacy restrictions that differ from state law.

> ***Key Takeaway: Delaware is more workable than many of its northeastern neighbors but is trending restrictive. The key advantages are open carry on foot, vehicle carry of loaded handguns in plain view or in the trunk, and conditional reciprocity with a meaningful number of states. The key cautions are the assault weapons and magazine bans (17-round limit), the felony classification for concealed carry without a license, and the increasingly restricted access to colleges and certain park facilities. If you're traveling through Delaware with a recognized permit, you can navigate the state with reasonable comfort — but verify your specific permit qualifies, leave restricted firearms and magazines at home, and pay attention to local postings.***
