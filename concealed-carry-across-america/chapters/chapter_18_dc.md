**Chapter 18**

**District of Columbia**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 5/100 — The most restrictive jurisdiction in the nation. Armed travel into or through the District should be avoided whenever possible.*

The District of Columbia presents the most hostile legal environment for firearms in the entire United States. Concealed carry permits are technically available but extraordinarily difficult to obtain. Open carry is completely prohibited. No out-of-state permits are recognized. Semi-automatic firearms classified as "assault weapons" and magazines holding more than 10 rounds are banned. Ammunition purchases require registration. Even transporting a lawfully owned firearm through the District demands strict compliance with federal safe-passage provisions, and enforcement is aggressive. For the traveling gun owner, the District should be treated as a legal minefield.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** All firearms must be registered; ammunition purchases require a valid registration certificate
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Military-pattern semi-automatics prohibited; magazines exceeding 10 rounds prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Not applicable — the District is a single jurisdiction
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited entirely — no exceptions
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; two-year terms; issuance is discretionary and extremely restrictive
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only (registered to the permit holder)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handguns must be unloaded, stored in a locked container separate from ammunition, and not readily accessible; long guns must be unloaded and cased
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Prohibited
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Not practically applicable
- **Parks and monuments:** Carry prohibited in all federal and District parks, memorials, and monuments
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry prohibited in all establishments that serve alcohol
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy; force-of-law posting provisions may apply
- **Airports:** Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport follows Virginia law in some areas; carry prohibited in terminal buildings
- **Highway rest areas:** Not applicable — the District has no highway rest areas

**For Permit Holders**

The District issues concealed carry permits to both residents and non-residents, but obtaining one requires navigating a complex application process through the Metropolitan Police Department. Applicants must demonstrate completion of approved firearms training, pass extensive background checks, and provide personal references. The licensing authority retains broad discretion in approving or denying applications.

Even with a valid District-issued permit, the list of prohibited locations is sweeping. Permit holders may not carry in government buildings, courts, schools, colleges, public transit facilities, stadiums, arenas, libraries, hospitals, emergency shelters, bars, restaurants serving alcohol, houses of worship, parks, memorials, monuments, museums, or any privately owned location that has not affirmatively consented to firearms. The practical effect is that a District permit authorizes carry on public sidewalks and in a limited number of private establishments that have explicitly opted in — an exceedingly narrow right.

The permit covers only handguns that are registered to the individual permit holder. Each firearm carried must be separately listed on the registration certificate.

**Without a Permit**

Travelers without a District-issued permit face extreme restrictions. Carrying a handgun — whether openly or concealed, on foot or in a vehicle — is a felony. The only lawful method of transport is under the federal McClure-Volkmer Act: the firearm must be completely unloaded, locked in a hard-sided case, stored in the trunk or in an area not readily accessible to any vehicle occupant, and ammunition must be stored separately. The traveler must be passing through the District en route to a lawful destination — not stopping for tourism, dining, or overnight stays.

District law enforcement is known to interpret these provisions strictly. Travelers who deviate from their route, make extended stops, or fail to meet any element of the safe-passage requirements risk felony prosecution and seizure of their firearms. Carrying even a single round of loose ammunition in a vehicle compartment accessible to passengers has resulted in arrests.

Long guns must be unloaded and stored in a case during any transport through the District. There are no practical exceptions for non-residents.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

The District bans military-pattern semi-automatic firearms, magazines holding more than 10 rounds, machine guns, suppressors, and bump stocks. All firearms possessed within the District must be registered with the Metropolitan Police Department. Ammunition may only be purchased by individuals who hold a valid registration certificate for a firearm chambered in that caliber.

There is no preemption issue because the District is a single jurisdiction, but federal property within the District — including the Capitol grounds, the National Mall, and all federal buildings — carries its own layer of firearms prohibitions enforced by federal law enforcement agencies.

> ***Key Takeaway: The District of Columbia should be avoided entirely by armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is banned, and even permit holders face restrictions so extensive that lawful carry is limited to public sidewalks and a handful of consenting private businesses. If your travel route passes through the District, follow strict McClure-Volkmer protocols: firearms completely unloaded, locked in a hard-sided case, stored in the trunk, with ammunition in a separate locked container. Do not stop for any reason beyond immediate emergencies. If the District is your destination, leave your firearms at home or consult a District-licensed attorney before making any other decision.***
