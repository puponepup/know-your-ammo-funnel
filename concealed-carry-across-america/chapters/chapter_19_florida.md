**Chapter 19**

**Florida**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 80/100 — A top-tier destination for armed travelers, with permitless carry, universal permit recognition, and strong preemption protections.*

As one of the most visited states in the country, Florida has made significant strides in recent years to accommodate gun owners. The adoption of permitless concealed carry allows any eligible individual aged 21 or older to carry a concealed firearm without obtaining a license. Combined with automatic recognition of all valid out-of-state permits, a robust Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, and a strong preemption law that punishes non-compliant local governments, the Sunshine State is among the most welcoming jurisdictions for armed citizens and travelers alike.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law with penalties for non-compliant local governments
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas; exceptions for fishing, hunting, and camping
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to both residents and non-residents; seven-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any eligible individual 21 or older with valid identification may carry concealed
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic recognition of all valid permits from every state, provided the holder is a resident of the issuing state and at least 21
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns, knives, stun guns, tear gas guns, and billies
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns permitted for individuals 21 and older; loaded long guns permitted for those 18 and older; persons under 21 must securely encase handguns
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner at all public universities
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Prohibited by most local school districts
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees and permitless carriers dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has established a prohibiting policy
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in passenger terminals; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Despite the availability of permitless carry, Florida continues to issue concealed weapon licenses to qualified individuals aged 21 and older. These seven-year licenses authorize the concealed carry of handguns, knives, stun guns, tear gas guns, and billies. Permits are available to both residents and non-residents.

The state recognizes all valid out-of-state carry permits, provided the holder is at least 21 years old (or at least 18 if a veteran or active-duty service member), is a resident of the state that issued the permit, and has the permit in their immediate possession.

Carrying firearms remains prohibited in numerous locations, including courthouses, police stations, polling places, meetings of governing bodies, K-12 schools, college campuses (beyond vehicles), career centers, mental health facilities, airport terminals, bars, establishments primarily devoted to the sale of alcohol, licensed public events, and places declared by law to be nuisances. Carry in houses of worship is permitted unless leadership has established a policy to the contrary.

**Without a Permit**

Any law-abiding individual aged 21 or older who is otherwise qualified to obtain a Florida permit and possesses valid identification may carry a concealed weapon without a license in most public areas. The firearm must remain concealed at all times — open carry on foot is not permitted under any circumstances except during lawful hunting, fishing, or camping activities.

Permitless carriers are subject to the same location-based restrictions as licensed individuals. Inside a vehicle, a loaded handgun may be carried on the person by anyone 21 or older with valid identification. Individuals aged 18 or older may transport loaded rifles or shotguns anywhere within a private vehicle. Persons under 21 who transport a handgun must securely encase it — acceptable methods include storing the firearm in a snapped holster (off the body), glove compartment, gun case, or other closed container.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Open carry while on foot is prohibited in all public areas throughout the state, regardless of license status. The only exceptions apply when a person is lawfully engaged in hunting, camping, or fishing.

Florida law protects the rights of individuals to store firearms in their locked vehicles while parked on company property — most businesses may not prohibit this practice for employees or customers. The state's strong firearm preemption law bars local governments from regulating the vast majority of firearm-related matters and imposes penalties for willful violations. Aside from certain zoning-related restrictions, firearm laws are uniformly regulated at the state level.

> ***Key Takeaway: Florida is one of the most accommodating states in the country for armed travelers, combining permitless carry, universal permit recognition, strong preemption, vehicle protection laws, and robust self-defense statutes. The primary restriction to remember is that open carry on foot is completely prohibited — your firearm must remain concealed at all times while in public. Additionally, while permitless carry is broadly available, maintaining a valid license still provides benefits for interstate reciprocity and the ability to carry a wider range of weapons beyond handguns.***
