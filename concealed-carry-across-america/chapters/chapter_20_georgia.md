**Chapter 20**

**Georgia**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — A solidly pro-gun state with constitutional carry, universal permit recognition, and strong preemption protections.*

Despite the growing urban influence of the Atlanta metropolitan area, Georgia remains firmly committed to firearms rights. The state's 2022 adoption of constitutional carry — combined with its recognition of all valid out-of-state permits held by non-residents — has cemented its position as one of the most welcoming states in the Southeast for armed citizens and travelers. A strong preemption law ensures that firearms regulations are applied uniformly statewide, and an enhanced Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections provides a robust legal framework for self-defense.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas, with some exceptions
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older (18 for active military) who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits held by non-residents from every state and jurisdiction are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and knives
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a private vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any lawful weapons carrier
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Georgia permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Handgun carry by lawful weapons carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Lawful weapons carriers may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the governing body of the institution has granted permission
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Georgia continues to issue weapons carry licenses through the probate court of the applicant's county of residence. These permits are available to qualified individuals aged 21 and older and are valid for five years. While the state does not issue permits to non-residents, it recognizes all valid out-of-state permits held by non-residents. Active-duty military personnel may use their military identification as a de facto carry license within the state.

Although not required for in-state carry due to constitutional carry provisions, Georgia licenses offer practical benefits. A formal permit provides a recognized credential when traveling to other states that honor Georgia licenses and helps satisfy federal requirements for carry within K-12 school zones.

**Without a Permit**

Individuals who qualify for — but do not possess — a weapons carry license may still carry a loaded firearm in their home, vehicle, place of business, and most public areas as "lawful weapons carriers." To qualify, a person must be at least 21 years old (or 18 if serving in the military) and must not be a convicted felon, an unlawful drug user, or someone currently admitted to a mental health or drug treatment facility.

Although Georgia requires in-state residency to obtain a weapons license, that requirement does not apply to permitless carry. As a result, qualified non-residents are also permitted to carry without a license under the state's constitutional carry provisions. Loaded firearms — including handguns, rifles, and shotguns — may be carried anywhere inside a private vehicle.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Despite broad carry permissions, several categories of locations remain off-limits. These include houses of worship (unless the governing body has granted permission), K-12 school safety zones, designated areas of college campuses (including student housing, childcare centers, and athletic facilities), courthouses, mental health facilities, areas within 150 feet of polling places during elections, nuclear power facilities, businesses that post no-carry signage, and government buildings that have security screening in place.

Concealed handgun carry is permitted in many outdoor areas of college campuses; however, it remains prohibited in most campus buildings. Parking areas for most restricted locations are generally accessible for firearm storage, provided the firearm remains locked inside the vehicle.

While handguns may be lawfully carried in parks, historic sites, recreational areas, and wildlife management areas, long guns in these settings should remain unloaded to comply with state laws governing hunting activities.

> ***Key Takeaway: Georgia is a thoroughly welcoming state for armed travelers. Constitutional carry extends to qualified non-residents, all out-of-state permits are recognized, vehicle carry is broadly permitted, and preemption ensures uniform laws statewide. The primary restrictions to be aware of are the house-of-worship prohibition (which defaults to "no carry" without explicit permission from church leadership), the campus building restrictions (outdoor areas are generally permissible for concealed handguns), and the school zone rules that make holding a formal permit advantageous despite permitless carry.***
