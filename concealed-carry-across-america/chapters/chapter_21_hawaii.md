**Chapter 21**

**Hawaii**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 5/100 — Among the most restrictive states in the nation. Armed travel to Hawaii is extremely difficult and strongly discouraged for all but the most determined permit holders.*

While a road trip to the islands is obviously not an option, travelers who fly to Hawaii with firearms will encounter one of the most hostile legal environments in the country. The state does not recognize any out-of-state carry permits, requires registration of all firearms within 72 hours of arrival, prohibits ammunition possession for individuals under 21, bans certain categories of semi-automatic pistols and high-capacity magazines, and maintains an exhaustive list of prohibited carry locations. Even with a Hawaii-issued permit, the locations where carry is actually lawful are so limited that the practical value of the license is negligible.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restricted; registration required within 72 hours of arrival; ammunition must be stored at home unless transported in a closed container to a firing range or repair facility
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Certain "assault pistols" prohibited; pistol magazines exceeding 10 rounds prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** No preemption statute; local governments may enact their own firearms ordinances
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas unless the individual holds a Hawaii open carry permit (rarely issued)
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued by local police chiefs to residents only; four-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Firearms prohibited in vehicles except during transport between authorized locations (home, firing range, police station, hunter education class, or repair facility)
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited on all campus property
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Prohibited on all campus property
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Immediately upon any official contact
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Loaded firearms carry and possession prohibited
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry prohibited wherever alcohol is served
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the institution has granted permission; local regulations may also apply
- **Hotels:** "Informed consent" rule applies — hotels must post signage explicitly allowing gun possession
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in all buildings, grounds, and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry prohibited in buildings

**For Permit Holders**

Hawaii issues concealed carry permits through county police chiefs to qualified residents who are at least 21 years old and are U.S. citizens or lawful permanent residents. Permits are valid statewide for four years and authorize the concealed carry of handguns only. Open carry permits technically exist but are effectively never issued.

Even with a valid permit, the list of prohibited locations is staggering. Permit holders are barred from carrying in government buildings, medical facilities, bars, restaurants serving alcohol, stadiums, theaters, libraries, colleges, K-12 schools, parks, playgrounds, polling places, transit hubs (airports, bus terminals), zoos, banks (though the bank ban has been temporarily suspended by court order), amusement parks, and public gatherings. In many cases, adjacent parking areas are included in these prohibitions. Private property — including most businesses — is presumed to prohibit firearms unless the owner has explicitly allowed carry through posted signage.

Firearms left in unattended vehicles must be secured in a locked, hidden "safe storage depository." Simply placing a firearm in the trunk or glove compartment does not meet this requirement.

**Without a Permit**

The environment for non-permitted individuals is effectively a complete prohibition. Carrying a firearm — either openly or concealed — on foot is strictly unlawful unless the individual is engaged in lawful hunting with the appropriate permit and an approved firearm.

In vehicles, firearms and ammunition are prohibited without a valid Hawaii permit. The only narrow exception permits the transport of unloaded, securely cased firearms between specific authorized locations: from the point of purchase to one's home, from home to a firing range, from home to a police station, from home to a formal hunter or shooter education class, or from home to a licensed repair facility. Ammunition may be transported under the same conditions in enclosed containers.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

All firearms brought into Hawaii must be registered within 72 hours of arrival, and the possessor must be at least 21 years old. Personal ownership of all Class III items — including machine guns — is strictly prohibited. The state also bans certain "assault pistols" and high-capacity pistol magazines capable of holding more than ten rounds.

Hawaii does not have a firearm law preemption statute, which means local counties and municipalities are free to enact their own carry restrictions. Many have done so, and these local ordinances are often not clearly posted. This lack of uniformity presents significant legal risks for gun owners — particularly those unfamiliar with the patchwork of local regulations.

Hawaii's laws provide only affirmative defenses for firearm possession in restricted areas. This means that even if you are ultimately found justified in possessing a firearm in a particular location, you may still be arrested, charged, and required to defend yourself in court.

> ***Key Takeaway: Hawaii should be treated as functionally off-limits for armed travelers. The state recognizes no out-of-state permits, requires registration of all firearms within 72 hours, prohibits carry in virtually every public location, presumes that private property prohibits firearms absent explicit signage, and lacks preemption — allowing a patchwork of local ordinances to further restrict gun owners. If you must travel to Hawaii with firearms, consult a Hawaii-licensed attorney before your trip, ensure all firearms are registered immediately upon arrival, and understand that practical carry opportunities will be essentially nonexistent.***
