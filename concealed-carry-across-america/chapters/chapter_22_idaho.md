**Chapter 22**

**Idaho**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 95/100 — One of the most permissive states in the nation, with permitless carry for all U.S. citizens 18 and older and universal permit recognition.*

Idaho's frontier heritage runs deep, and the state's firearms laws reflect an enduring commitment to individual liberty. Permitless concealed carry is available to any U.S. citizen aged 18 or older — one of the lowest age thresholds in the country. The state recognizes all out-of-state permits, maintains strong preemption protections, and imposes very few restrictions on where firearms may be carried. For armed travelers exploring the northern panhandle, the Rockies, or the wide-open spaces of the Snake River Plain, Idaho presents one of the most straightforward legal environments in the nation.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard and enhanced permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any U.S. citizen 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every other state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful deadly weapon (broader than handguns only)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded deadly weapons — including handguns, rifles, and shotguns — may be carried anywhere in a motor vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for enhanced permit holders and retired law enforcement officers
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Idaho permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by U.S. citizens 18 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** U.S. citizens 18 and older may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Statutory allowance for guest rejection or ejection for firearm possession
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Idaho issues both standard and enhanced concealed weapons permits through county sheriffs to qualified individuals aged 21 and older. Both permit types are valid for five years and authorize the carry of most lawful deadly weapons — a broader allowance than states that limit carry to handguns. Individuals aged 18 to 20 may obtain provisional permits, which can be upgraded to enhanced permits upon renewal at age 21. Non-residents who hold a valid carry license from their home state are eligible for the enhanced permit.

The enhanced permit provides additional privileges beyond what standard permits and permitless carry offer. Enhanced permit holders may carry on college and university campuses — a privilege not available to standard permit holders or permitless carriers. Many other states also recognize only the enhanced Idaho permit for reciprocity purposes, making it a valuable credential for interstate travel.

**Without a Permit**

Idaho permits any U.S. citizen aged 18 or older who meets the legal qualifications for obtaining a permit — that is, no disqualifying factors such as felony convictions — to carry a concealed deadly weapon without a license. This applies both on foot and in vehicles.

When on foot, concealed carry is allowed in all areas except those specifically restricted. In vehicles, firearms may be carried either openly or concealed in any location — including under the seat, in the dashboard, glove compartment, center console, or trunk.

Open carry remains legal statewide for individuals 18 and older. The combination of permitless concealed carry and unrestricted open carry means that the vast majority of public spaces are accessible to armed individuals without any license requirement.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Despite the broad permissiveness, firearms are prohibited in specific locations including jails, courthouses, and K-12 schools. Private businesses that display signage banning weapons may enforce their policies. State universities may restrict firearms on their campuses through policies set by their governing boards, though enhanced permit holders and retired law enforcement officers are exempted from most campus restrictions.

Idaho's preemption law prevents local governments from regulating most aspects of firearm ownership and carry. While property owners may still prohibit firearms on their premises, the state provides some protections for individuals storing firearms in vehicles on employer parking lots — including shielding employers from liability related to employee firearm storage.

Private event organizers using public property may not restrict attendees from carrying firearms unless the event is closed to the public, charges admission, or is by invitation only. This provision protects armed individuals attending public gatherings held on government land.

> ***Key Takeaway: Idaho is one of the most permissive states in the country for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, universal permit recognition, unrestricted vehicle carry, strong preemption, and minimal prohibited locations make it an exceptionally straightforward environment to navigate while armed. The primary reason to obtain a permit — particularly the enhanced version — is for reciprocity benefits when traveling to other states and for access to college campuses. Hotels present a notable exception to the general permissiveness: state law specifically allows hotels to reject or eject guests for firearm possession, so check hotel policies before your stay.***
