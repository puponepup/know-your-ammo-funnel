**Chapter 23**

**Illinois**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 20/100 — Highly restrictive, with no out-of-state permit recognition, an extensive list of prohibited locations, and bans on semi-automatic firearms and high-capacity magazines.*

Illinois presents one of the more challenging legal landscapes for armed travelers. The state does not recognize any out-of-state concealed carry permits, prohibits open carry entirely, requires residents to obtain a Firearm Owner's Identification (FOID) card before possessing firearms or ammunition, bans semi-automatic firearms classified as "assault weapons," and restricts magazine capacity. A limited vehicle carry provision for non-residents with home-state permits provides a narrow pathway, but carrying on foot without an Illinois-issued license is prohibited.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restricted; FOID card required for residents to possess firearms or ammunition
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** "Assault weapons" prohibited; magazine capacity limited to 10 rounds for long guns and 15 rounds for handguns
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Partial preemption — handgun regulation is preempted; some cities (Chicago, Cicero, Thornton) maintain local restrictions on items such as laser sights
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; stand-your-ground protections not codified in statute
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Licenses issued to residents and non-residents from select states (Arkansas, Idaho, Mississippi, Nevada, Texas, Virginia); non-resident fee is $300
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Non-residents with a valid home-state carry permit may carry a concealed, loaded handgun inside their vehicle; upon exiting, the firearm must be stored in a locked compartment of the locked vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy for permit holders
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Illinois permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Some exemptions for permit holders, but posted lot restrictions may apply
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by Illinois licensees permitted in most outdoor areas; prohibited inside buildings and within refuges and nature preserves
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for license holders dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited on all airport property; vehicle carry permitted if the firearm is in a closed compartment of a locked vehicle
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry prohibited in buildings and on grounds; vehicle carry by permit holders permitted

**For Permit Holders**

Illinois issues concealed carry licenses to qualified residents who hold a valid FOID card. The state also extends license eligibility to non-residents from a handful of states — Arkansas, Idaho, Mississippi, Nevada, Texas, and Virginia — for a $300 fee. Licenses authorize the concealed carry of handguns only.

Even with a valid license, the list of prohibited locations is extensive. Carry is banned in childcare facilities, government buildings (including highway rest areas), courthouses, K-12 schools, hospitals, nursing homes, public transportation, bars, licensed public events, playgrounds, local parks and athletic fields, colleges, casinos, stadiums, amusement parks, airports, libraries, museums, zoos, and any private property that bans firearms. All restricted areas must be clearly posted with standardized signage.

While most outdoor areas on state-managed land — including state parks — permit concealed carry for licensees, firearms are not allowed inside buildings or within state refuges and nature preserves. In any restricted area, permit holders must store their firearms in a locked vehicle, either in the trunk or glove compartment.

**Without an Illinois Permit**

A non-resident who is legally authorized to carry a firearm in public in their home state may carry a concealed, loaded handgun inside their vehicle while traveling through Illinois. This includes the passenger area of a car or a locked container on a motorcycle. However, if the non-resident exits the vehicle, the vehicle must be locked and the firearm stored in a closed compartment.

Long guns must be unloaded and kept in a case while inside a vehicle, regardless of permit status. Non-residents without a valid carry permit must also store all firearms and ammunition in the trunk.

Open carry is prohibited statewide. Semi-automatic firearms classified as "assault weapons" and magazines exceeding 10 rounds (long guns) or 15 rounds (handguns) are banned. While Illinois allows residents to retain restricted items that were owned prior to the bans, non-residents may only transport such firearms through the state for up to 24 hours — and only if they are unloaded, cased, and stored separately from ammunition. There are no specific transport exceptions for non-residents carrying high-capacity magazines, and travelers are strongly advised to leave these items at home.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Illinois requires all residents to possess a FOID card before owning firearms or ammunition. The state bans semi-automatic firearms with military-style features, magazines exceeding the specified capacity limits, machine guns, and suppressors.

While handgun regulation is preempted at the state level, some municipalities maintain additional restrictions. Chicago and several surrounding cities impose ordinances that may exceed state-level restrictions on items such as laser sights and other accessories.

> ***Key Takeaway: Illinois is a difficult state for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is prohibited, and the list of location-based restrictions for permit holders is one of the longest in the country. The saving grace for non-residents is the vehicle carry provision: if you hold a valid carry permit from your home state, you may keep a concealed, loaded handgun in your vehicle while passing through. But the moment you step out, the firearm must go into a locked compartment of a locked vehicle. Leave restricted firearms, high-capacity magazines, and suppressors at home. If Illinois is your destination and you want to carry on foot, you must apply for an Illinois license — and only residents of a handful of states are even eligible.***
