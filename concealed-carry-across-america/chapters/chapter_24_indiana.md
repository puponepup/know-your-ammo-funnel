**Chapter 24**

**Indiana**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — One of the most permissive states in the country, with permitless carry, universal permit recognition, and strong preemption protections.*

Indiana has one of the oldest firearm carry laws in the United States. Since 1935, both residents and non-residents with regular employment or business in the state have been eligible to obtain licenses to carry loaded handguns for personal protection. The state's recent adoption of permitless carry legislation expanded these rights further, allowing any eligible individual aged 18 or older to carry a handgun — either openly or concealed — without a license. Combined with universal permit recognition, a strong Castle Doctrine, and a preemption law that imposes significant penalties on non-compliant local governments, Indiana ranks among the most welcoming states for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; localities may be sued for violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas, with some exceptions
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Five-year and lifetime licenses issued to residents and qualifying non-residents
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every state and jurisdiction are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; loaded long guns generally permitted, but must be cased and unloaded on DNR property
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Indiana permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Handgun carry by any person 18 or older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Persons 18 and older may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition; churches located on school property are also permitted
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Although Indiana now allows permitless carry, the state continues to issue handgun licenses that remain valid for five years. These licenses authorize both concealed and open carry and are available to Indiana residents and to non-residents who work within the state. Residents also have the option to apply for lifetime carry licenses, which remain valid permanently.

Non-residents who hold valid handgun permits from other states are automatically granted recognition in Indiana. However, they must adhere to the specific conditions of their out-of-state permits. For example, if a permit restricts carry to concealed only, the firearm must remain concealed — even though Indiana generally permits open carry. This distinction has become less significant with the adoption of permitless carry.

Maintaining a formal license still provides practical benefits for interstate travel, as many other states recognize Indiana permits. The lifetime license, in particular, eliminates the burden of periodic renewals.

**Without a Permit**

Any individual aged 18 or older who is not otherwise prohibited from possessing a firearm may legally carry a handgun — either openly or concealed — in most public places. Carrying a loaded handgun in a vehicle is also permitted, with lawful placement allowed anywhere within the passenger compartment or trunk.

Vehicle carry of loaded long guns is generally permitted throughout the state. However, on property owned by the Indiana Department of Natural Resources, long guns must be unloaded and cased. Long guns transported on ATVs or snowmobiles must be unloaded and cased regardless of location, although carrying a loaded handgun in these vehicles is permitted.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited in riverboat casinos, K-12 schools (except when attending a worship service at a church located on school property), the state fairgrounds, the Indiana Government Center, horse racing tracks, shipping ports, and Falls of the Ohio State Park. At K-12 schools, casinos, and the state fairgrounds, firearms may be stored securely in the glove compartment or trunk of a locked vehicle. At the Government Center, racing tracks, shipping ports, and Falls of the Ohio State Park, vehicle storage is not permitted.

Federal law further restricts possession of a loaded handgun within a K-12 school zone — including inside a vehicle — unless the individual holds a valid resident carry license. This is a significant reason to maintain a formal permit even in a permitless carry state.

Indiana's preemption law largely eliminates local regulation of carry laws. While local governments may restrict firearms in courthouses, hospitals, and during certain special events, any attempt to regulate carry elsewhere can result in significant legal penalties against the locality.

Employees in most businesses are legally permitted to store firearms in their vehicles while parked on company property, provided the firearm is kept out of sight in the glove compartment or trunk. This protection applies to employees but does not necessarily extend to other visitors such as customers or guests, who may be subject to posted no-firearms policies.

> ***Key Takeaway: Indiana is one of the easiest states to navigate while armed. Permitless carry at 18, universal permit recognition, open and concealed carry without restriction, broad vehicle carry rights, and strong preemption create an environment where armed travelers can move freely with minimal legal complexity. The main reason to maintain a formal license is for K-12 school zone compliance under federal law and for reciprocity benefits when traveling to other states. Be aware of the specific locations where carry is fully prohibited — particularly the Government Center, racing tracks, and Falls of the Ohio State Park — where even vehicle storage is not permitted.***
