**Chapter 25**

**Iowa**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — A top-tier pro-gun state with constitutional carry, universal permit recognition, and a strong preemption law with enhanced penalties for non-compliant local governments.*

Iowa's adoption of constitutional carry placed the state firmly among the most firearms-friendly jurisdictions in the Midwest. While Iowans have long maintained a strong cultural connection to firearms — rooted in generations of hunting tradition and rural self-reliance — the new framework reinforces those values with legal protections that benefit both residents and visitors. Universal permit recognition, a strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions, and a preemption law that allows citizens to sue non-compliant local governments round out a highly favorable environment for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine gun ownership prohibited; suppressors lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; localities may be sued for violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas under the constitutional carry law
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Non-professional licenses issued by county sheriffs to residents; professional licenses available to residents and non-residents through the Department of Public Safety
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits held by non-residents from every state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any rifle, shotgun, handgun, knife, taser, or stun gun
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried openly or concealed; rifles and shotguns should remain cased and unloaded (a statutory conflict with the permitless carry law remains unresolved)
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited by law
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Generally prohibited; unloaded firearms may be stored in a locked container in the passenger compartment or trunk
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees and those 21 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees and those 21 and older dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Statutory allowance for guest rejection or ejection for firearm possession
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Iowa issues two types of carry licenses. Non-professional licenses are granted through the sheriff of the applicant's home county to qualified residents aged 21 and older. These five-year licenses authorize both open and concealed carry of any firearm in most public locations. Professional licenses are issued to residents through their local sheriff and to non-residents through the Iowa Department of Public Safety. Professional licenses are available to individuals 18 and older whose need to carry a firearm arises from their employment.

While permitless carry has eliminated the legal requirement to obtain a license, maintaining a permit provides several practical advantages — including easily verifiable proof of lawful carry during law enforcement encounters and expanded reciprocity when traveling to other states. Iowa recognizes all valid out-of-state permits held by non-residents.

**Without a Permit**

Any law-abiding individual aged 21 or older may carry a loaded handgun — either in a vehicle or on foot — in most public places. Loaded rifles and shotguns may also be carried on foot under the permitless carry law. However, existing conservation statutes still prohibit loaded long guns in vehicles without a permit, creating a statutory conflict that has not yet been formally resolved. Travelers are advised to keep long guns unloaded and cased while in vehicles until this issue is clarified.

Permitless carriers should avoid all designated gun-free zones and are legally required to cooperate with law enforcement if their conduct while armed gives rise to a reasonable suspicion that they may pose a danger to themselves or others.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited in casinos, K-12 schools, the state fairgrounds, courthouses, and state universities. Concealed handguns — if not openly displayed — are permitted inside the State Capitol. Firearm carry, whether open or concealed, is allowed while operating ATVs and snowmobiles.

Iowa law prohibits most individuals from possessing machine guns. The only exception applies to historical reenactors using firearms classified as "curios and relics," provided the weapons have been rendered inoperable.

> ***Key Takeaway: Iowa is an excellent state for armed travelers, combining permitless carry, universal permit recognition, a broad weapons allowance under its licensing system, and strong preemption protections. The main caution is the unresolved conflict between the permitless carry law and existing conservation statutes regarding loaded long guns in vehicles — until this is formally resolved, keep long guns unloaded and cased during vehicle transport. Hotels are another area to watch, as state law specifically allows hotels to reject or eject guests for firearm possession.***
