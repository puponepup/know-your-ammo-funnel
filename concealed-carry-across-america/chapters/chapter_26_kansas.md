**Chapter 26**

**Kansas**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — Among the best in the Midwest, with permitless carry, universal permit recognition, strong preemption, and a culture deeply rooted in firearms heritage.*

Kansas has long been recognized for maintaining some of the most favorable firearms laws in the country. The state's permitless carry provisions, universal recognition of out-of-state permits, strong preemption law, and vehicle protection statutes create an environment where armed citizens and travelers can navigate with confidence. Historical events — such as the armed citizens of Coffeyville who successfully defended their town against the Dalton Gang in 1892 — speak to a deep tradition of self-reliance that continues to shape the state's legal framework.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful under state law; localities may restrict carry only in certain government buildings
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard licenses issued by the Attorney General to residents only; four-year terms; provisional licenses available to residents aged 18-20
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 21 and older may carry without a license
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; keeping a handgun in a locked vehicle is always lawful, including in parking lots of restricted buildings
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Kansas permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by citizens 21 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Concealed carry permitted if the premises are not posted
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Kansas issues standard concealed carry licenses to residents aged 21 and older through the Attorney General's office. These licenses are valid for four years. Provisional licenses are also available to residents aged 18 to 20, allowing them to carry concealed handguns — sometimes with additional privileges, such as the ability to carry at the State Capitol.

Although individuals 21 and older no longer need a permit to carry within Kansas, obtaining a license provides important benefits — particularly reciprocity in other states and the ability to carry in certain government buildings that require a formal license. Kansas recognizes all valid out-of-state permits, provided the holder is not a Kansas resident.

Concealed carry may be restricted in both public and private buildings, but only if proper signage approved by the Attorney General is posted or if adequate security measures (such as screening stations) are in place. State facilities may also regulate how firearms are carried — for example, by requiring that handguns remain holstered. Importantly, these restrictions apply only to buildings, not to parking lots. Keeping a handgun in a locked vehicle in a parking lot is always lawful. Violations of posted carry restrictions are typically treated as civil offenses, not criminal.

**Without a Permit**

Individuals aged 21 and older may carry a loaded handgun anywhere in their vehicle without a permit. Both open and concealed carry on foot are generally permitted in most public spaces. Hunters and anglers are specifically protected under state law, allowing them to carry firearms while engaged in their activities.

Most local regulations that previously restricted carry have been repealed under the state's preemption statute. However, both private and government property owners retain the right to prohibit firearms within their buildings, provided they post appropriate signage.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Certain restricted areas require special attention. The State Capitol and state government offices permit only the concealed carry of handguns, and only by licensed individuals or those 21 and older. Some universities may impose additional restrictions, such as requiring firearms to have an empty chamber while on campus. Tribal lands, including casinos, typically honor concealed carry licenses but may not extend the same rights to unlicensed individuals. Regulations can vary between reservations, so travelers should check local policies before visiting.

> ***Key Takeaway: Kansas is one of the most straightforward states for armed travelers. Permitless carry, universal permit recognition, strong preemption, and vehicle protection laws create a legal environment where carrying is simple and predictable. The key nuances are the building-versus-parking-lot distinction (restrictions apply only inside posted buildings, never in parking lots), the civil rather than criminal nature of posted-signage violations, and the variation in regulations on tribal lands. Obtaining a formal license remains valuable for interstate reciprocity and for carry in government buildings that require one.***
