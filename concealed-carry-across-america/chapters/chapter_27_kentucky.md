**Chapter 27**

**Kentucky**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — A deeply pro-gun state with one of the highest per-capita gun ownership rates in the nation, permitless carry, universal permit recognition, and robust preemption protections.*

Kentucky's relationship with firearms is as fundamental as its bourbon and bluegrass. The state boasts one of the highest rates of per-capita gun ownership in the country, and its legal framework reflects an uncompromising commitment to Second Amendment rights. The adoption of constitutional carry in 2019 allowed any eligible individual aged 21 or older to carry a concealed firearm without a license. Combined with universal permit recognition, a strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions, and a preemption law that imposes severe penalties on non-compliant local governments, the Bluegrass State is among the most welcoming in the nation for armed citizens and travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; localities may be sued for violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Licenses issued by the State Police through county sheriffs to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits held by non-residents from every state are honored
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful deadly weapon (broader than handguns only)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Kentucky permit holders (non-students) may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and those 21 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Persons 21 and older may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

The Kentucky State Police issue concealed carry licenses for firearms and other deadly weapons through the sheriff of the applicant's home county. These licenses are valid for five years. While Kentucky does not issue permits to non-residents, it honors any valid out-of-state permit, provided the holder is not a Kentucky resident. Recognized permit holders may carry concealed in most public locations.

Carry is prohibited in certain locations regardless of permit status, including childcare centers, K-12 schools, bars, legislative sessions, law enforcement agencies, and courthouses. Colleges, hospitals, local government buildings, and private businesses may also prohibit firearms on their premises by posting appropriate signage. Notably, violating these posted bans is not considered a criminal offense under state law — it is treated as a civil matter.

**Without a Permit**

Since the enactment of constitutional carry in 2019, Kentucky no longer requires a license to carry a concealed firearm. Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle — whether stored in a console box, glove compartment, under the seat, or openly displayed in a belt holster. These carry methods are lawful for individuals who do not hold a formal permit.

On foot, permitless concealed carry is permitted in most areas where licensed carry is allowed. Permitless carriers must follow the same location restrictions as license holders. Open carry of a firearm while on foot is also broadly permitted in most public areas. Carrying in a visible belt holster secured on the hip is the recommended method.

One notable distinction: carrying a firearm in a vehicle on K-12 school grounds requires a Kentucky-issued license to comply with federal school zone regulations. This is a significant reason to maintain a formal permit even in a permitless carry state.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Kentucky's robust preemption law imposes strong penalties on any locality that attempts to regulate firearm carry beyond what state law permits. This ensures that firearms regulations are applied uniformly across the state, eliminating the patchwork of local ordinances that complicates travel in other jurisdictions.

Vehicle carry of firearms is broadly protected, including on private property. Property owners may not prevent individuals from storing firearms in their vehicles while parked on their premises.

> ***Key Takeaway: Kentucky is one of the most welcoming states in the nation for armed travelers. Permitless carry, universal permit recognition, unrestricted vehicle carry, strong preemption, and a culture that deeply embraces firearms ownership make it an exceptionally easy state to navigate while armed. The primary reason to maintain a formal license is for K-12 school zone compliance and interstate reciprocity. Posted no-carry signage violations are civil matters — not criminal offenses — under state law, which provides an additional layer of protection for carriers who may inadvertently enter a posted location.***
