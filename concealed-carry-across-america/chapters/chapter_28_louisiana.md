**Chapter 28**

**Louisiana**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — A solidly pro-gun state with permitless carry starting at age 18, strong preemption protections, and a deeply rooted firearms culture.*

Known as the "Sportsman's Paradise," Louisiana has long maintained a deep respect for firearm ownership and outdoor traditions. The recent passage of permitless carry for individuals 18 and older has solidified the state's position among the most gun-friendly jurisdictions in the nation. A strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions, a preemption law that has been updated to eliminate grandfathered local ordinances, and broad vehicle carry protections round out a highly favorable legal environment for armed citizens and travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine gun ownership restricted to "war relic" models; suppressors lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; localities may be sued for violations; pre-1985 grandfathered ordinances have been repealed
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued by the Department of Public Safety to residents only; five-year and lifetime options available
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states that reciprocally recognize Louisiana permits; holder must be at least 21 and not a Louisiana resident
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner (vehicles only; campus buildings restricted)
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Louisiana permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Immediately upon any official contact — proactive disclosure required
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and those 18 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Persons 18 and older may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the institution's leadership has granted explicit permission
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and possible force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Louisiana issues concealed handgun permits through the Department of Public Safety to qualified residents aged 21 and older. Applicants may choose between a five-year license or a lifetime license. While the state does not issue permits to non-residents, it recognizes valid permits from other states that offer reciprocal recognition to Louisiana permit holders. Out-of-state permit holders must be at least 21 and must not reside in Louisiana.

Both Louisiana permit holders and recognized non-resident permit holders may carry in most public areas, subject to the location-based restrictions outlined below.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone aged 18 or older in Louisiana may legally carry a loaded handgun — openly or concealed — while on foot in most public areas. Permitless carriers must follow the same location restrictions that apply to permit holders and are prohibited from carrying while under the influence of alcohol or drugs.

Individuals 18 and older may carry a firearm anywhere inside a vehicle, including in glove compartments, console boxes, and seat pockets. Open carry in plain view — such as in a snapped holster — is also permitted. Loaded long guns may be stored in commercial gun cases, gun racks, or external storage compartments.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms may not be carried in police stations, jails, courthouses, polling places, parades and demonstrations, K-12 schools, the State Capitol, public buildings used for government meetings, bars, and many areas of college campuses. Private businesses may ban firearms inside their buildings by posting proper signage, though this restriction does not apply to parking lots.

Houses of worship present a specific restriction: carry is permitted only if the church leader gives explicit permission. Private residences are also off-limits for carry without the homeowner's consent.

College campuses are generally restricted, but individuals may keep firearms in their private vehicles. Students may store firearms in dormitory rooms if they have received institutional approval.

Louisiana law requires anyone carrying a firearm to immediately inform a law enforcement officer upon contact that they are armed — regardless of whether the officer asks. This proactive duty-to-inform requirement is one of the strictest in the nation and sets Louisiana apart from most other states.

The state's strong preemption law bars local governments from regulating most aspects of firearm possession, carry, and transport. Recent updates have eliminated grandfathered local ordinances enacted before 1985 and provide for legal action against municipalities that violate state preemption.

> ***Key Takeaway: Louisiana is a highly favorable state for armed travelers, with permitless carry at 18, strong preemption, vehicle protection laws, and a culture that embraces firearms ownership. The two critical items to remember are the proactive duty-to-inform requirement — you must immediately disclose to any law enforcement officer that you are armed, before being asked — and the house-of-worship restriction that defaults to "no carry" without explicit permission from church leadership. Machine gun ownership is limited to war relics, so travelers with Class III items should verify that their specific firearms qualify.***
