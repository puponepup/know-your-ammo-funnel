**Chapter 29**

**Maine**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — One of New England's most permissive states, with permitless carry, strong preemption, and a backcountry culture that rivals Alaska in its embrace of firearms freedom.*

Maine's rugged landscapes and self-reliant culture have fostered an enduring respect for firearms rights. The adoption of permitless concealed carry secured the Pine Tree State's position as one of the most firearm-friendly states in the nation — and the clear standout in the New England region. Strong preemption protections ensure uniform regulations statewide, and a robust Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions provides a solid legal foundation for self-defense.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful for all individuals while on foot
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Licenses issued to residents and non-residents; four-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older (18 for active-duty military) who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states that reciprocally recognize Maine permits
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Anyone 21 or older may carry a loaded handgun anywhere in a vehicle; loaded long guns are prohibited in vehicles for everyone — magazines may be loaded but must not be attached to the firearm
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Permitless carriers must immediately inform officers upon contact; permit holders are not required to volunteer this information
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted; most state parks require a license (Baxter State Park prohibits firearms entirely)
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Concealed carry permitted if the premises are not posted
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Statutory allowance for guest rejection; force-of-law posting prohibitions may apply
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking lots of most airports; Augusta state airport prohibits carry on its grounds through administrative rule
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Maine issues licenses for carrying a loaded handgun — either concealed on one's person or in a vehicle — to individuals 18 years or older. Licenses are valid for four years. Residents apply through their local police chief, while non-residents apply directly to the State Police. Maine recognizes permits from states that offer reciprocal recognition, allowing those permit holders to carry concealed, loaded handguns statewide.

Permit holders enjoy several advantages over permitless carriers. They are not required to proactively disclose their armed status during law enforcement encounters (permitless carriers are). Permit holders may also carry concealed handguns in Acadia National Park and most state parks — locations where permitless carriers are restricted. Baxter State Park prohibits firearms entirely, regardless of permit status.

Carry is prohibited in casinos, courthouses, K-12 schools, posted private properties, wildlife sanctuaries, labor dispute sites, much of the State Capitol, and restaurants that serve alcohol and have posted no-carry signs.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone aged 21 or older may carry a loaded handgun anywhere inside their vehicle — whether in the glove compartment, console, under the seat, or on their person. Active-duty military personnel aged 18 to 21 with official identification are also permitted to carry. On foot, open carry of a loaded handgun is allowed for all individuals, and those 21 and older (including qualified military members) may carry concealed handguns without a permit. No other weapons may be carried concealed without a license.

Individuals carrying concealed handguns without a permit are required to immediately inform a law enforcement officer upon any official contact. This notification requirement is not imposed on permit holders — creating a meaningful practical distinction between licensed and unlicensed carry.

In addition to the locations restricted for all carriers, permitless carriers are also prohibited from carrying in Acadia National Park and all state parks — which generally require a license for handgun carry.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Maine prohibits carrying any loaded long gun in a vehicle. This applies to everyone — including permit holders. Rifles and shotguns must remain unloaded during vehicle transport. While magazines may be loaded, they cannot be attached to or inserted in any firearm during transport. This restriction is absolute and represents one of the few areas where Maine's laws are more restrictive than those of neighboring permitless carry states.

> ***Key Takeaway: Maine is an outstanding state for armed travelers, particularly those arriving from other parts of New England. Permitless carry, open carry on foot, broad vehicle handgun carry, and strong preemption create a highly favorable environment. The key distinctions to remember are: (1) the loaded long gun prohibition in vehicles applies to everyone; (2) permitless carriers must proactively inform law enforcement, while permit holders do not; and (3) state parks and Acadia National Park generally require a formal license for concealed carry. Baxter State Park prohibits all firearms. These differences provide strong motivation to obtain a permit even though one is not required for basic carry.***
