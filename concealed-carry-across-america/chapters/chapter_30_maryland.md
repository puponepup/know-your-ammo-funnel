**Chapter 30**

**Maryland**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 15/100 — Highly restrictive with no out-of-state permit recognition, strict transport rules, magazine capacity limits, and an expanding list of prohibited carry locations currently facing legal challenges.*

Maryland's regulatory approach to firearms has grown increasingly restrictive in recent years. A sweeping 2023 law effectively banned the carrying of firearms in most public places, though enforcement of many of its provisions has been temporarily deferred by the courts. The state does not recognize any out-of-state permits, prohibits open carry entirely, restricts the sale and importation of military-pattern semi-automatic firearms, and limits magazine capacity. Local governments retain broad authority to impose additional carry restrictions, creating a patchwork of regulations that complicates travel for armed citizens.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No restrictions on possession; sales of all firearms heavily regulated
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Importation of "assault weapons" prohibited; transfer or sale of magazines exceeding 10 rounds prohibited; possession and importation of existing magazines not expressly regulated
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** State registration required for machine guns; suppressors lawful with proper federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Limited preemption law; local carry restrictions are common
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; two-year terms (three years for renewals)
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Generally limited to handguns and knives
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded, readily accessible handguns prohibited; limited exceptions for unloaded transport in secure cases to specific activities; rifles and shotguns must remain unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Locked in a container is lawful; also subject to school policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited at public schools
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Possession and carry of firearms prohibited except at designated ranges
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry prohibited per 2023 law (enforcement currently deferred by court order)
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Statutory allowance for guest rejection or ejection for firearm possession
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in buildings and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Open display of firearms prohibited in buildings and on grounds; concealed carry by permit holders permitted

**For Permit Holders**

Maryland issues carry permits to both residents and non-residents. Permits are valid for two years (three years for renewals). Carriers must keep their permit on them at all times and may only carry concealed — open carry is not permitted. The state does not recognize handgun permits issued by any other state.

Even with a valid permit, carry is prohibited in numerous locations including K-12 schools, legislative buildings, certain hotels, government-owned buildings, childcare and preschool centers, state parks and forests, transit and port facilities, airports, stadiums, polling places, museums, casinos, racetracks, healthcare facilities, colleges, and amusement parks. While limited exceptions exist for storing firearms in vehicles, they do not apply universally.

Additional restrictions include bans on carrying at public demonstrations, in bars and restaurants that serve alcohol, and in private businesses unless those businesses have clearly posted signs allowing carry. However, enforcement of carry bans in some locations — particularly bars, restaurants, and private businesses — has been temporarily deferred by the courts and is subject to ongoing legal challenges.

**Without a Maryland Permit**

Individuals without a Maryland permit may not carry a handgun — either on foot or in a vehicle — in a loaded or readily accessible manner. However, they are permitted to transport an unloaded handgun in a secure case when traveling to or from specific activities such as sport shooting events, gun shows, hunting trips, target practice, repair shops, or places of purchase.

Magazines may remain loaded in the vehicle as long as they are stored separately from the firearm. Travelers merely passing through Maryland are permitted to transport unloaded, inaccessible handguns — the firearm must be unloaded, secured in a case, and stored in the trunk. If the vehicle has no trunk, the handgun must be locked in a case. Ammunition should be stored separately from the firearm.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Rifles and shotguns may be transported in a vehicle for any lawful reason. However, they must be unloaded and secured in commercial gun cases or racks, regardless of permit status.

While the state has a limited preemption statute, it allows local governments to impose additional carry restrictions. Montgomery County has been notably aggressive, recently prohibiting carry in nearly all public venues. Travelers should exercise extreme caution and research local ordinances before carrying in any Maryland municipality.

> ***Key Takeaway: Maryland is a difficult state for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is prohibited, vehicle carry without a permit is limited to unloaded firearms in locked cases traveling to specific activities, and the list of prohibited locations for permit holders is extensive and growing. The 2023 carry ban is partially suspended by court order, creating legal uncertainty about which restrictions are currently enforceable. Montgomery County's local restrictions are particularly aggressive. If you must transport firearms through Maryland, follow strict McClure-Volkmer protocols and do not deviate from your route.***
