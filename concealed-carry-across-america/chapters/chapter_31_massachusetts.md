**Chapter 31**

**Massachusetts**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 10/100 — One of the most restrictive states in the nation. Mandatory licensing for all firearm and ammunition possession, severe penalties for violations, and no out-of-state permit recognition.*

Massachusetts enforces some of the strictest firearms laws in the country. Possession of rifles, shotguns, handguns, or ammunition requires a state-issued Firearm Identification Card or License to Carry. Violations of state gun laws carry a mandatory minimum prison sentence of one year. The state does not recognize any out-of-state permits, bans all semi-automatic "assault weapons" and magazines holding more than 10 rounds, prohibits personal ownership of suppressors, and requires new residents and most non-residents to register their firearms before bringing them into the state. For armed travelers, Massachusetts demands extreme caution and meticulous compliance.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restricted; Firearm Identification Card required for all possession
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Importation of any "assault weapon" or magazine exceeding 10 rounds is prohibited; residents may possess pre-1994 magazines on private property only
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine gun ownership requires state permit and federal compliance; personal ownership of suppressors prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Firearms laws are uniformly strict throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas unless the individual possesses a Massachusetts permit (though strongly discouraged even for permit holders)
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Licenses issued to residents (six-year terms) and non-residents (one-year temporary permits for $100)
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and certain rifles and shotguns
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handgun carry generally prohibited; standard non-semi-automatic rifles and shotguns must be unloaded and in locked containers
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited by law
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by Massachusetts licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and possible force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in terminal areas and parking facilities (security zone enforcement)
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry by Massachusetts permit holders permitted in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Massachusetts issues licenses to carry to residents aged 21 and older. These six-year licenses authorize the concealed carry of handguns in most public locations. The state also offers temporary one-year permits to non-residents for $100, which require in-state training and an in-person application through the Firearms Records Bureau.

Permit holders may carry in most public places but are prohibited from carrying in government offices, polling stations, K-12 schools, colleges, airports, court facilities, casinos, nature preserves, and any location where carry is explicitly prohibited by signage. When carrying in a vehicle, the handgun must be under the exclusive control of the permit holder. While open carry is not technically prohibited for permit holders, it is strongly discouraged and may result in unwanted law enforcement attention.

**Without a Massachusetts Permit**

Carrying a handgun in a vehicle without a Massachusetts license is prohibited. A recent legislative change appears to permit non-residents who are simply "traveling through" the state to possess a handgun in their vehicle; however, enforcement guidelines for this exemption have not been finalized. Travelers are strongly advised to transport firearms in strict accordance with the federal McClure-Volkmer Act.

Non-residents may transport unloaded, non-semi-automatic rifles, shotguns, and ammunition in locked containers while traveling through the state. Hunters from out of state may carry these firearms if they possess a valid Massachusetts non-resident hunting license. Most other long gun possession by non-residents beyond traveling through or hunting requires a separate identification permit.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Massachusetts permanently bans all semi-automatic "assault weapons," magazines holding more than 10 rounds, and bump stocks from entering the state. Residents may possess pre-1994 high-capacity magazines on private property, but non-residents are prohibited from bringing these items into Massachusetts under any circumstances.

New residents — and non-residents who are not simply passing through — are required to register their firearms before bringing them into the state. There are no exceptions for competitive shooters. Violations of state firearms laws carry mandatory minimum sentences, making inadvertent non-compliance an extremely high-stakes risk.

> ***Key Takeaway: Massachusetts is one of the most hostile states in the nation for armed travelers. Mandatory licensing for all possession, no out-of-state permit recognition, a one-year mandatory minimum sentence for violations, bans on semi-automatic firearms and standard-capacity magazines, prohibition of suppressor ownership, and registration requirements for new residents create a legal environment where even minor compliance failures can result in felony charges. If you must transport firearms through Massachusetts, follow strict McClure-Volkmer protocols and do not stop. If Massachusetts is your destination, apply for a non-resident temporary permit well in advance of your trip. Leave all semi-automatic firearms, standard-capacity magazines, and suppressors at home.***
