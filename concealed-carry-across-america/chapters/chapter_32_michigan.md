**Chapter 32**

**Michigan**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 55/100 — A mixed environment. Residents enjoy reasonable carry rights with a strong Castle Doctrine, but non-residents without permits face significant restrictions.*

Michigan's scenic northern landscapes and outdoor traditions attract many gun enthusiasts, but visitors should be aware that the state's firearms laws are more restrictive than they might expect. While residents benefit from a strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, a preemption law that ensures uniform regulations statewide, and broad open carry rights, the state does not offer permitless carry and imposes significant limitations on non-residents who lack a home-state carry permit. Handgun registration is required at the point of purchase, adding another layer of complexity for travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Firearm registration required at time of purchase; no ammunition restrictions
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Folding-stock rifles under 26 inches require NFA registration; no general magazine capacity restrictions
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas; non-residents must hold a home-state permit
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Concealed Pistol Licenses issued by county clerks to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Recognizes resident permits from all states; holder must be at least 21
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and stun guns
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Long guns must be transported unloaded and encased or secured in the trunk; non-residents may not transport handguns without a home-state permit
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Michigan permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Immediately upon any official contact
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless church leaders grant permission
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Michigan issues Concealed Pistol Licenses through county clerks to qualified residents only. These five-year licenses also exempt the holder from needing separate purchase permits for future handgun acquisitions. Michigan recognizes valid carry permits issued by all other states, provided the holder is a resident of the issuing state and at least 21 years old.

Permit holders may not carry a concealed, loaded handgun in certain locations including K-12 schools, sports arenas, large entertainment venues, churches (unless authorized by church officials), hospitals, bars, casinos, daycare centers, and universities. However, vehicle carry is permitted in the parking lots of these restricted locations — except for casinos. Open carry by permit holders is legal in most public areas, again excluding casinos.

Permit holders must be the legal owners of any handgun they carry and are required to immediately inform law enforcement that they are carrying during any official contact. This proactive duty-to-inform is one of the strictest in the nation.

**Without a Permit**

Michigan residents without a carry permit may transport a handgun for a "lawful purpose," provided the firearm is unloaded, securely cased, and stored in the trunk or a location that is not easily accessible. "Lawful purpose" includes transportation to recreational activities such as shooting ranges or hunting locations. The handgun must also be registered with the Michigan State Police — a requirement imposed at the time of acquisition.

Non-residents face additional hurdles. A non-resident may not transport a handgun in Michigan unless they hold a carry permit from their home state. Without a home-state permit, even unloaded, cased handgun transport is legally problematic.

Michigan allows the open carry of a loaded handgun while on foot in most public areas, except in locations such as K-12 schools, banks, churches, courts, theaters, sports arenas, daycare centers, hospitals, and alcohol-serving businesses. Open carry without a permit is limited to Michigan residents. Non-residents who wish to open carry must hold a valid home-state permit. When open carrying, the handgun should be kept in a visible holster. Individuals carrying without a permit should not enter a vehicle while openly carrying — doing so requires a valid concealed pistol license.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Long guns must be transported unloaded and encased or secured in the trunk, regardless of permit status. The state's preemption law ensures that firearms regulations are applied uniformly statewide, eliminating the local-ordinance patchwork found in many other states.

Michigan's handgun registration requirement at the point of purchase presents a unique consideration for travelers. Because all handguns must be registered with the State Police, non-residents who bring handguns into the state should ensure they can demonstrate lawful ownership and carry authorization.

> ***Key Takeaway: Michigan is a workable but nuanced state for armed travelers. The state recognizes resident permits from all other states, allows open carry for permit holders, and maintains strong preemption and Castle Doctrine protections. However, the absence of permitless carry, the handgun registration requirement, the strict duty-to-inform during law enforcement encounters, and the significant restrictions on non-residents without home-state permits make preparation essential. If you plan to visit Michigan with a handgun, verify that you hold a valid resident permit from your home state — without one, your options are extremely limited.***
