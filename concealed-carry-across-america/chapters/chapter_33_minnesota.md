**Chapter 33**

**Minnesota**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 45/100 — A moderately restrictive state with conditional permit recognition, no permitless carry, and strict transport requirements for non-permittees. Permit holders enjoy reasonable carry rights.*

Minnesota imposes a range of restrictions on firearms that may surprise visitors from more permissive states. While the state issues carry permits to both residents and non-residents and maintains a preemption law that ensures uniform regulations statewide, it does not offer permitless carry, prohibits open carry without a valid permit, and requires all firearms to be transported unloaded and cased by non-permittees. Permit recognition is conditional — only permits from states with similar standards are honored, as determined by the Department of Public Safety.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restrictions on possession; handgun sales require a permit or background check
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Possession lawful; "assault weapon" sales require a permit
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine gun ownership restricted to curios and relics only; suppressors lawful; bump stocks and "trigger activators" prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** State preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas unless the individual possesses a recognized permit
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — only permits from states with similar standards are recognized, as approved by the Department of Public Safety
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** All firearms must be securely cased and unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner (vehicles only)
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Minnesota permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle; permit holders may also exit the vehicle to retrieve a firearm from the trunk or rear storage area
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Most parking lot owners may not prohibit firearms in vehicles
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Churches may prohibit carry on all church property without the need for posted notice (per court ruling)
- **Hotels:** Statutory allowance for guest rejection or ejection for firearm possession
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Minnesota issues carry permits to both residents and non-residents who are at least 21 years old. Residents apply through the sheriff of their home county, while non-residents may apply through any sheriff's office in the state. Permits are valid for five years and authorize both concealed and open carry of handguns.

Permit holders may carry in most public areas, including state parks and most forested areas. However, loaded firearms are not permitted within designated wildlife refuges. Universally prohibited locations include K-12 schools, childcare facilities, and courthouses where judicial orders prohibit firearms. Private businesses and public institutions — including universities — may restrict firearms inside their buildings if they post signs or enforce policies. However, with the exception of churches, these restrictions generally cannot extend to parking areas.

A court ruling allows churches to prohibit firearms on all church property — including parking lots — without the need for posted notice. This is a significant exception to the general rule that private property restrictions are limited to the interior of buildings.

**Without a Permit**

Non-permittees face strict transport requirements. Handguns transported in a vehicle must be unloaded and fully enclosed in a closed commercial gun case. This requirement applies whether the firearm is stored in the passenger compartment or the trunk — the weapon must always remain both unloaded and properly cased.

When on foot, individuals may transport handguns without a permit between their residence and their place of business. Those engaged in lawful hunting or target shooting may also carry handguns while actively participating in those activities. Outside of these specific exceptions, carrying a loaded handgun — whether openly or concealed — is restricted to individuals with a valid carry permit.

Rifles and shotguns transported in a vehicle must be unloaded and secured in commercial gun cases, unless stored in the trunk (where they must still be unloaded but are not required to be cased). These transport rules apply to everyone — both permit holders and non-permittees — because Minnesota's carry permit authorizes only the carry of handguns, not long guns.

Open carry of long guns on foot is generally prohibited, except when actively engaged in lawful hunting or target shooting. In all other public settings, long guns must be unloaded and properly cased.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Minnesota restricts machine gun ownership to curios and relics. The state has also enacted legislation expanding the definition of prohibited "trigger activators" — which includes bump stocks and similar devices. Suppressors remain lawful with proper federal compliance.

The state's preemption law ensures uniform firearms regulations, but the conditional nature of permit recognition means that not all out-of-state permits are honored. Travelers should verify with the Minnesota Department of Public Safety that their specific permit qualifies before relying on reciprocity.

> ***Key Takeaway: Minnesota is a moderately restrictive state that requires more preparation than its Midwestern neighbors. There is no permitless carry, and non-permittees must transport all firearms unloaded and cased. Permit recognition is conditional — not all out-of-state permits are honored, so verify yours before traveling. Permit holders enjoy reasonable carry rights in most public locations, but should be aware of the church exception (churches may prohibit carry on all property without notice) and the long gun transport rules (which apply to everyone, including permit holders). If you plan to visit Minnesota without a recognized permit, your firearms must remain unloaded and cased at all times.***
