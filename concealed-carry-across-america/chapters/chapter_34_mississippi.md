**Chapter 34**

**Mississippi**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — A deeply pro-gun state with constitutional carry, universal permit recognition, an enhanced permit option, and strong cultural support for firearms rights.*

Mississippi's firearms laws reflect a strong heritage of gun ownership that is deeply woven into the state's cultural identity. Constitutional carry provisions allow individuals to carry loaded, concealed handguns in vehicles and in holsters on foot without a license. The state also offers both standard and enhanced permits, with the enhanced version granting access to most otherwise-restricted locations. Universal recognition of all out-of-state permits, a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, and vehicle protection statutes round out a highly favorable legal environment.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, though cities may restrict carry in parks and at public meetings
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas; some local restrictions possible
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard and enhanced permits issued by the Department of Public Safety to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older may carry a concealed handgun in a vehicle or in a holster on foot
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** All valid permits from every state are honored (treated as equivalent to regular Mississippi permits)
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and stun guns
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded, concealed handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; long guns should be unloaded during hunting season
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any non-student gun owner
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Mississippi non-student permit holders may carry a pistol in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms stored in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and those exercising constitutional carry permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless the individual holds an enhanced permit or serves as a designated security officer
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in terminals except for enhanced permit holders; parking facilities lawful for all
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds unless posted

**For Permit Holders**

Mississippi issues concealed carry licenses to residents for five-year terms. These permits can be upgraded to "enhanced" status with additional training, allowing the holder to carry in most otherwise-restricted areas — excluding jails, police stations, declared nuisance properties, and some posted locations. While the state does not issue permits to non-residents, it recognizes all out-of-state permits as equivalent to regular Mississippi permits.

Permit holders may carry their handguns either openly or concealed. Individuals with a regular permit are prohibited from carrying at athletic events, airport terminals, police stations or jails, government meetings, courthouses, colleges, bars, churches, parades, polling places, places of nuisance, and on properties where carry is expressly forbidden by posted signage.

Enhanced permit holders enjoy significantly broader access, including churches and airport terminals — locations that remain off-limits to standard permit holders and permitless carriers.

**Without a Permit**

A license is not required to carry a loaded, concealed handgun in a vehicle or in a fully enclosed container or holster while on foot. Acceptable containers include any closed purse, handbag, satchel, or briefcase. Acceptable holsters include any belt or shoulder holster worn on the person. The handgun must remain secured in the holster and may not be carried loosely — such as tucked into a waistband or belt without a holster.

Open carry of handguns is also permitted while on foot. If the firearm is not in a belt or shoulder holster, it must remain clearly visible and not concealed from public view. Concealed carry without a permit is additionally lawful for individuals engaged in target shooting, hunting, or fishing.

Individuals using the permitless carry option are subject to the same location restrictions that apply to regular permit holders.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Standard-length long guns may be carried openly in most public places and transported in vehicles. During deer and turkey seasons, hunting regulations mandate that any long gun in a vehicle or carried on foot along a public road must be unloaded. At other times of year, long guns may be loaded. This seasonal restriction applies equally to permit holders and permitless carriers.

Businesses may only ban firearms inside their buildings — locking a firearm in a vehicle on their parking lot remains lawful. Municipalities may impose carry restrictions in specific areas such as public parks or meetings, but state preemption statutes penalize any city that attempts overly broad regulation.

> ***Key Takeaway: Mississippi is one of the most accommodating states in the South for armed travelers. Constitutional carry, universal permit recognition, vehicle protection laws, and the enhanced permit option create a flexible and favorable legal environment. The key distinctions are between regular and enhanced permits: the enhanced version — which requires additional training — unlocks access to churches, airport terminals, and most other restricted locations. For maximum flexibility, obtaining or upgrading to an enhanced permit is well worth the effort. Travelers should also note the seasonal long gun restriction during deer and turkey seasons.***
