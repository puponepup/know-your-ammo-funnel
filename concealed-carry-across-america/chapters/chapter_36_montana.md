**Chapter 36**

**Montana**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — Big Sky Country lives up to its reputation with permitless carry, broad open carry protections, strong preemption, and a deeply ingrained firearms culture.*

Montana's wide-open landscapes provide travelers with stunning scenery and good reason to carry a firearm for personal protection. The state's deep ties to the Old West are reflected in its modern permitless carry law, which allows individuals to carry concealed without a permit in both rural areas and within city limits. Strong preemption protections, a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions, and legal safeguards against local government overreach make Montana one of the most firearms-friendly states in the West.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; some local regulation of public buildings possible; individuals may sue localities that infringe on carry rights
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted; local governments may restrict open carry only within publicly owned or occupied buildings
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard and enhanced permits issued to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states that conduct criminal background checks prior to issuance; holders must carry photo identification if permit lacks a photo
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns, knives, and any lawful deadly weapon
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college policy (Board of Regents retains authority)
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Montana permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Open or concealed carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Prohibited from barring gun possession by guests
- **Airports:** Localities may restrict open carry by anyone and concealed carry by non-permittees in airport buildings; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Montana issues carry licenses through local sheriffs to qualified residents. Residents aged 18 to 20 may apply for a "temporary restricted" permit, which can be upgraded to an "enhanced" permit at age 21. All permits are valid for five years.

Montana does not issue permits to non-residents but recognizes out-of-state permits from jurisdictions that conduct criminal background checks prior to issuance. Non-resident permit holders must also carry photo identification if their permit does not include a photo.

Permit holders may carry concealed in most public spaces. However, carry is prohibited in detention centers, law enforcement buildings, federal facilities, military bases, secure airport areas, courtrooms, K-12 school buildings, and any privately owned property that is clearly posted against firearms.

**Without a Permit**

Montana permits any individual who is legally allowed to possess a firearm to carry it concealed without a permit. This applies in most public areas, except in state and local government offices and publicly owned buildings where local authorities have opted to restrict unlicensed concealed carry. All locations that are off-limits to permitted carriers remain restricted for permitless carriers as well.

Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere within a vehicle, including in the glove compartment, under the seat, or in the trunk.

Open carry is broadly permitted and protected under state law. Local governments may restrict individuals from openly carrying firearms only within publicly owned or occupied buildings. Outside of such buildings — including in parks and at public gatherings — local restrictions on open carry are not permitted.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Montana provides strong protections against local overreach. Individuals whose carry rights are infringed by a locality may file suit and seek damages. Local officials who enforce preempted restrictions may face significant legal liability.

Hotels are prohibited from barring gun possession by guests — a notable protection that distinguishes Montana from many other states.

College campus carry remains complex. Although the permitless carry law initially extended to campuses, a subsequent court ruling upheld the Board of Regents' authority to set firearms policies for state universities. As a result, individual colleges may impose their own restrictions.

> ***Key Takeaway: Montana is an outstanding state for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, broad open carry protections, strong preemption with legal remedies for individuals, vehicle carry freedoms, and the unique hotel protection that prohibits hotels from barring armed guests all contribute to one of the most permissive legal environments in the country. The enhanced permit provides additional reciprocity benefits for interstate travel and may be recognized by states that do not honor the standard permit. College campuses are the primary area of complexity — check individual institutional policies before carrying on campus.***
