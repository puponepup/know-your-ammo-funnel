**Chapter 37**

**Nebraska**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 80/100 — A recently improved environment with permitless carry, strong preemption, and a frontier spirit that reflects the state's independent character.*

Rooted in small-town values and a strong respect for tradition, Nebraska has taken a significant step in affirming Second Amendment rights with its recent adoption of permitless carry. In keeping with the state's independent spirit and frontier legacy, individuals — whether residents or visitors — are no longer required to obtain a permit to carry a concealed handgun in most public areas. Combined with universal permit recognition, improved preemption protections, and a straightforward legal framework, Nebraska has established itself as a welcoming destination for armed travelers.

**For Permit Holders**

Nebraska issues concealed carry permits to both residents and non-residents aged 21 and older. These five-year licenses authorize the concealed carry of handguns in most public locations. The state also recognizes all valid permits from every other state.

Permit holders enjoy access to locations that may be restricted for permitless carriers, including certain government buildings and facilities. The permit also serves as proof of lawful carry authorization during law enforcement encounters and provides reciprocity benefits for interstate travel.

**Without a Permit**

Any individual aged 21 or older who is legally entitled to possess a firearm may carry a concealed handgun in most public locations without obtaining a permit. Loaded handguns may also be carried anywhere inside a vehicle by individuals 21 and older.

Long guns must be unloaded during vehicle transport, regardless of permit status. Open carry on foot is lawful for individuals meeting the age requirement.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Nebraska's improved preemption law prohibits local governments from regulating gun carry and possession, ensuring uniform laws across the state. However, handgun sales still require a purchase permit — a restriction that primarily affects residents rather than travelers.

Firearms are prohibited in courthouses, police stations, K-12 schools, and other locations designated by state law. Private property owners and businesses may prohibit firearms on their premises through posted signage.

> ***Key Takeaway: Nebraska has significantly improved its legal environment for armed travelers with the adoption of permitless carry and strengthened preemption protections. The state now offers a straightforward framework: individuals 21 and older can carry concealed handguns in most public areas and in vehicles without a license. The handgun purchase permit requirement affects buyers rather than travelers, and universal permit recognition ensures that visitors with existing licenses can carry with confidence. Keep long guns unloaded during vehicle transport and watch for posted signage at private businesses and restricted government facilities.***
