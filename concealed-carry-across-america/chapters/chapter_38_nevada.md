**Chapter 38**

**Nevada**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 60/100 — Historically gun-friendly, but recent legislative trends suggest a shift. No permitless carry, though open carry remains broadly permitted and vehicle carry is relatively permissive.*

Nevada has historically maintained a gun-friendly environment, particularly in its rural regions. However, recent legislative changes — including bump stock bans and child access prevention laws — signal an evolving political landscape. The state does not offer permitless carry, but it issues concealed carry permits to both residents and non-residents, allows open carry in most public areas, and maintains a strong preemption law that recently invalidated all local firearms ordinances, including Clark County's former handgun registration requirement.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; all local ordinances eliminated except discharge restrictions; localities may be sued for violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and commonly accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states with similar regulations
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried openly or in the glove compartment or console, but may not be concealed on the person; rifles and shotguns must be unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited without written permission from the institution
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Prohibited without written permission from the school
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permitted for licensees dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in passenger terminals; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Concealed handgun carry by permit holders permitted in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Nevada issues concealed carry licenses to both residents and non-residents who are at least 21 years old, or between 18 and 20 if actively serving in the armed forces. Residents apply through their local sheriff, while non-residents may apply through any county sheriff. Licenses are valid for five years and cover handguns only.

Nevada recognizes permits from states with similar firearms regulations. Licensed individuals may carry throughout the state, except in K-12 schools, childcare facilities, universities, secure facilities, public airports, and public buildings that have either posted signage or security screening prohibiting firearms.

**Without a Permit**

Travelers may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle if the firearm is either in plain view or secured in the glove compartment or console. Concealed carry on one's person without a permit is prohibited — this includes firearms hidden under clothing or in bags. However, a loaded handgun stored in a visible gun case on an empty passenger seat is permitted.

On foot, individuals without a permit may openly carry a handgun — such as in a visible, snapped belt holster. Open carry is more common and accepted in rural areas than in urban settings.

Rifles and shotguns may be transported in vehicles as long as they are unloaded. "Unloaded" means no live round in the chamber, though magazines may remain loaded.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

The state's expanded preemption law prevents local governments from enforcing their own firearms regulations — including Clark County's former handgun registration requirement, which is no longer valid. The only local authority retained is the ability to regulate the discharge of firearms within city limits. Any locality attempting to enforce firearms restrictions in violation of this law faces civil liability.

> ***Key Takeaway: Nevada remains a workable state for armed travelers, particularly those with a recognized permit. Open carry is broadly allowed, vehicle carry of loaded handguns is permitted in plain view or in the glove compartment, and the recent expansion of the preemption law has eliminated local ordinances that previously complicated travel. However, the absence of permitless carry means that concealed carry on the person requires a license. Travelers should verify that their out-of-state permit is recognized before relying on reciprocity, and should be aware that colleges and K-12 schools require written permission for even vehicle-based firearm possession.***
