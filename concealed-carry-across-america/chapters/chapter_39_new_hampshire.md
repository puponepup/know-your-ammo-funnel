**Chapter 39**

**New Hampshire**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — "Live Free or Die" is more than a motto. Permitless carry, strong preemption, broad open carry, and minimal restrictions make the Granite State a standout in the typically restrictive Northeast.*

New Hampshire's iconic motto was powerfully affirmed in 2017 when the state enacted constitutional carry. Despite its location in the typically restrictive Northeast, the Granite State has firmly positioned itself among the most gun-friendly states in the country. Strong preemption protections, a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provisions, and a commitment to minimal government intrusion make New Hampshire an outstanding destination for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Strong preemption law; firearms regulations uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states with reciprocity agreements
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried concealed or openly anywhere in a vehicle; rifles and shotguns must remain unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** New Hampshire permit holders may carry a loaded handgun
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises; employee vehicle storage is protected by law
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Although no longer required for carrying a loaded handgun, New Hampshire continues to issue concealed carry licenses. Residents apply through the police chief or sheriff of their hometown, and permits are valid for five years. Non-residents may obtain permits through the New Hampshire State Police for $100, and a home-state permit is no longer required as a prerequisite.

While constitutional carry is in effect, formal licenses still offer significant advantages for those who travel to other states that require a permit. The State Police actively maintain and establish reciprocity agreements to maximize the permit's utility beyond state borders.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone who is not legally prohibited from possessing a firearm may carry a loaded handgun in a vehicle without a permit. The handgun may be carried openly, concealed on the person, or stored in a glove compartment, console box, or gun case. Recent changes also permit the carry of loaded handguns while operating snowmobiles or ATVs.

On foot, carrying a loaded handgun — either openly or concealed — is allowed in most public areas except for specific restricted locations. New Hampshire's strong preemption law prevents local governments from regulating firearm possession, transport, or carry, ensuring consistent laws statewide.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded when transported in a vehicle and should be stored in a gun case, gun rack, or the vehicle's trunk. Magazines may remain loaded but must not be attached to the firearm. This restriction applies to everyone, including recognized license holders.

Carrying firearms is generally permitted in most public areas. Prohibited locations include courthouses, correctional facilities, childcare centers, residential care facilities, and any property that is clearly posted against firearms.

K-12 school properties are notably not off-limits to carry for individuals who possess a valid New Hampshire permit and are not students. A recent opinion from the state Attorney General affirmed that local school districts do not have the authority to impose independent firearm bans.

New Hampshire law protects employees' rights to store firearms in their vehicles while parked on their employer's property. This protection is limited to employees and does not extend to other visitors such as customers or guests.

> ***Key Takeaway: New Hampshire is the clear standout in the Northeast for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, broad open carry, unrestricted vehicle handgun carry, strong preemption, and a minimal list of prohibited locations create one of the most favorable legal environments in the country. The long gun transport restriction (unloaded and cased in vehicles) is the primary limitation. Obtaining a formal permit remains valuable for interstate reciprocity, and the non-resident permit is accessible and reasonably priced.***
