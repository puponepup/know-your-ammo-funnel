**Chapter 40**

**New Jersey**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 5/100 — Among the most restrictive states in the nation. No out-of-state permit recognition, severe penalties, hollow point ammunition banned, and an exhaustive list of prohibited locations.*

New Jersey enforces some of the most restrictive firearms laws in the country. Its dense urban population and prevailing political climate have produced a highly regulated environment for gun ownership and carry. The state does not recognize any out-of-state permits, prohibits open carry entirely, bans hollow point handgun ammunition, restricts magazine capacity to 10 rounds, prohibits semi-automatic "assault weapons" and .50 caliber rifles, and requires new residents to register their firearms. The list of prohibited carry locations is so extensive that even permit holders have extremely limited options for lawful carry.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restricted; carry permit required for handgun possession outside the home; Firearm Purchaser Identification Card required for long guns; hollow point handgun ammunition prohibited; handgun ammunition registered at purchase; new residents must register firearms
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** "Assault weapons" and magazines exceeding 10 rounds banned from import; .50 caliber rifles prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** No state preemption; local carry laws exist and may be severe
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; two-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized under any circumstances
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Firearms may not be carried in a vehicle without a Purchaser Identification Card or NJ carry permit; narrow exceptions for unloaded, cased transport to specific activities
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited by law
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Immediately upon any official contact
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Firearms carry prohibited
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Firearms carry prohibited
- **Houses of worship:** Carry may be prohibited by local ordinance; otherwise permitted unless posted
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in buildings and on grounds
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry by New Jersey permit holders permitted if not posted

**For Permit Holders**

New Jersey requires a license to carry a loaded handgun either on one's person or inside a vehicle. Residents apply through their local police departments, while non-residents apply directly to the State Police. Licenses are valid for two years and authorize concealed carry only.

Even with a valid permit, public carry is significantly limited. Restricted areas include most government buildings, courthouses, jails, polling places, permitted public gatherings, K-12 schools, colleges, childcare centers, zoos, museums, parks, beaches, entertainment venues, youth sporting events, libraries, bars and restaurants serving alcohol, casinos, airports, transit hubs, and healthcare facilities.

Transporting a firearm in restricted areas — or when leaving a vehicle unattended — is permitted only if the firearm is out of sight and stored in a locked container. New Jersey also requires express permission (via posted signs) from private property owners for firearms to be carried on their premises. However, enforcement of this rule and the requirement that handguns must be unloaded and cased while in a vehicle has been temporarily paused by a court ruling.

**Without a New Jersey Permit**

Carrying a handgun outside of one's home or place of business requires a state-issued carry permit. Possession of rifles and shotguns requires a Firearm Purchaser Identification Card. This ID card or a carry permit is also necessary for transporting firearms in a vehicle.

Narrow exceptions allow individuals to transport unloaded and cased firearms without an ID card or permit when traveling directly to a hunting trip, firearms exhibition, repair shop, or target shooting range. General firearm transport throughout the state without appropriate documentation is prohibited. Travelers who do not possess a New Jersey ID card should strictly adhere to federal McClure-Volkmer Act transport protocols.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Private ownership of military-style semi-automatic firearms, bump stocks, magazines holding more than 10 rounds, machine guns, .50 caliber rifles, and most types of hollow point handgun ammunition is banned. Residents may possess hollow point ammunition within their homes and may permanently modify magazines to comply with the 10-round limit. Non-resident travelers may not bring any restricted items into the state.

Without a preemption statute, local governments are free to impose additional firearms regulations. Many municipalities have enacted severe local ordinances that further restrict carry beyond state law.

> ***Key Takeaway: New Jersey is one of the most hostile states in the nation for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is prohibited, hollow point ammunition is banned, magazines are limited to 10 rounds, semi-automatic "assault weapons" are prohibited, and the list of location-based restrictions is exhaustive. The absence of preemption means local ordinances may impose even stricter rules. If your route requires passing through New Jersey, follow strict McClure-Volkmer protocols and do not stop. If New Jersey is your destination, leave all restricted items at home — including hollow point ammunition and standard-capacity magazines — and understand that even with a permit, lawful carry opportunities are extremely limited.***
