**Chapter 41**

**New Mexico**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 55/100 — A mixed environment rooted in frontier tradition but shaped by increasing urban political influence. Vehicle carry is unrestricted, but on-foot concealed carry requires a permit.*

New Mexico's rugged mountains, sweeping prairies, and deep frontier heritage have long made gun ownership a part of everyday life. Open carry is broadly permitted, and vehicle carry of loaded firearms is unrestricted. However, the state does not offer permitless carry for on-foot concealed carry, issues permits to residents only, and has seen increasing regulatory pressure from urban areas — particularly Albuquerque, which has begun enforcing local carry bans that may conflict with the state's preemption statute.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, but some local rules persist in Albuquerque
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; stand-your-ground protections not codified in statute
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; four-year terms; limited to one handgun at a time
- **Permitless carry:** No (on-foot concealed carry requires a permit; vehicle carry is unrestricted)
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states with established reciprocity agreements; holder must be at least 21
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Limited to one handgun at a time
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded, concealed firearms may be carried anywhere in a private vehicle for self-protection
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for anyone over 19; subject to college policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** New Mexico permit holders may carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry in establishments serving beer and wine (unless posted); establishments serving hard liquor are off-limits to everyone
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted, though contradictory (and potentially unlawful) signage may exist

**For Permit Holders**

New Mexico issues concealed carry licenses to state residents who are at least 21 years old and U.S. citizens. Permits are valid for four years. The state does not issue permits to non-residents but recognizes valid permits from other states that have established reciprocity agreements.

Permit holders are limited to carrying only one concealed handgun at a time. Concealed carry is prohibited in locations listed in the universal restrictions section, but permit holders are allowed to carry in restaurants that serve beer and wine — unless the establishment is clearly posted to prohibit firearms — and in retailers that sell alcohol for off-premises consumption, such as liquor stores and grocery stores. These alcohol-related locations remain off-limits to individuals without a valid carry permit.

**Without a Permit**

Vehicle carry of firearms is unrestricted in New Mexico. Individuals may carry loaded, concealed firearms in the passenger compartment or trunk of a vehicle. Acceptable storage locations include under the seat, inside a glove compartment, or concealed in a purse or bag.

On foot, non-permittees may carry an unloaded firearm anywhere on their person, including in a concealed manner using a belt holster, gun case, or similar method. Loaded handguns may be openly carried in most public areas. However, non-permittees are prohibited from carrying firearms — loaded or unloaded — in establishments that sell alcohol and in several other restricted locations. Violating these restrictions is a felony offense.

Non-permittees are also prohibited from carrying a firearm within 100 feet of a polling place unless they remain entirely within their vehicle.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited on public buses, in game preserves, on college campuses, in preschools, at K-12 schools, in courthouses, on tribal lands (unless explicitly permitted by the governing tribe), in restaurants serving hard liquor, in the State Capitol, and on any private property clearly posted to prohibit firearms.

While the state's preemption law generally bars local governments from enacting their own firearms regulations, Albuquerque is currently enforcing local bans on carry in playgrounds, parks, and government buildings. These local restrictions face ongoing legal challenges.

New Mexico has a unique age restriction for handgun possession: individuals must be at least 19 years old to legally possess a handgun, which differs from the 18-year-old threshold in most other states.

> ***Key Takeaway: New Mexico offers a split personality for armed travelers. Vehicle carry is completely unrestricted — you can carry loaded, concealed firearms anywhere in your car — making the state very workable for travelers passing through. However, on-foot concealed carry requires a permit, and the state only issues permits to residents. Visitors with recognized out-of-state permits should verify reciprocity before relying on their license. Open carry on foot is broadly permitted for loaded handguns, but pay close attention to the alcohol-related location restrictions: carrying in a bar or hard-liquor restaurant without a permit is a felony. Albuquerque's local restrictions add another layer of complexity in that metro area.***
