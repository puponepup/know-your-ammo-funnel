**Chapter 42**

**New York**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 5/100 — Among the most restrictive states in the nation. No out-of-state permit recognition, an exhaustive list of "sensitive locations," and a separate permit requirement for New York City create an extremely hostile environment for armed travelers.*

In 2022, the Supreme Court struck down New York's requirement that license applicants demonstrate a "special need" for self-defense. The state's response was swift and aggressive: it enacted what has become one of the strictest carry laws in the country. Although this new law faces ongoing legal challenges, its practical impact has been severe — making carrying a firearm in public nearly unattainable even for permit holders. The state does not recognize any out-of-state permits, requires a separate city-issued permit for carry within New York City, prohibits open carry entirely, bans semi-automatic "assault weapons" and magazines exceeding 10 rounds, and designates an extraordinary number of public locations as "sensitive" zones where carry is a felony.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** Restricted; permit required for handgun possession; ammunition sales restricted but possession is not; all firearms in New York City require a separate city-issued permit; semi-automatic rifle acquisition requires a license
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** "Assault weapons" and magazines exceeding 10 rounds may not be imported
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** No preemption law; local regulation — especially in New York City — adds additional layers of restriction
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and some non-residents; licenses do not expire but must be recertified every three years
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized (except New York City pistol licenses, which are valid statewide)
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded; handgun carry requires a New York license; all firearms in unoccupied vehicles must be unloaded and cased
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited by law
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Firearm possession or use prohibited; limited exceptions for "special permit" hunting
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry prohibited in any business where alcohol is served
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited for all except authorized security officers
- **Hotels:** Hotels must post signage explicitly allowing carry; otherwise firearms are prohibited
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in passenger terminals and parking areas
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry prohibited in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

New York mandates a license for both possession and carry of a handgun. These licenses are available to residents aged 21 and older who complete required training. Non-residents who are primarily employed in the state or maintain part-time residence may also be eligible. Licenses do not technically expire but must be recertified every three years and authorize concealed carry only.

Even with a valid license, the list of prohibited locations is staggering. Designated "sensitive locations" include government buildings, healthcare facilities, schools, colleges, airports, subways, bus and train stations, polling places, theaters, stadiums, casinos, places of worship, outdoor events, Times Square, bars, most dining establishments, zoos, libraries, playgrounds, parks, racetracks, and museums. Carrying in these locations is subject to felony charges.

Private businesses are also considered off-limits unless the proprietor posts signage explicitly allowing firearms — though a federal court invalidated this provision in October 2024 (this ruling may be appealed or reversed). Carry within New York City requires a separate, special permit issued by the City.

**Without a New York Permit**

New York does not recognize permits issued by any other state. Travelers may only transport handguns through the state under strict McClure-Volkmer Act compliance: firearms must be unloaded and locked in a secure case placed in the trunk or designated storage compartment. The traveler's final destination must be a state that allows possession without a license. Carrying documentation such as hotel reservations to verify the interstate purpose of the journey is strongly recommended.

Standard rifles and shotguns may be transported in a vehicle provided they are unloaded, with ammunition stored separately, and secured in gun cases. If the vehicle is left unattended, all firearms must be unloaded and stored in hard-sided, locked cases or safes kept out of sight.

Possession of any firearm within New York City requires a separate city-issued permit, so travelers without one should avoid bringing firearms into the city entirely.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Importing machine guns, semi-automatic "assault weapons," or magazines holding more than 10 rounds is prohibited. A handgun may be considered "loaded" under New York law if ammunition is nearby — even if not physically inserted in the firearm.

The absence of a preemption statute means that local governments — most notably New York City — may impose additional restrictions beyond state law.

> ***Key Takeaway: New York is one of the most hostile states in the nation for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, open carry is banned, the list of "sensitive locations" where even permit holders face felony charges is extraordinary in its breadth, private businesses must affirmatively opt in to allow carry, New York City requires its own separate permit, and standard-capacity magazines are prohibited. If your route passes through New York, follow strict McClure-Volkmer protocols: firearms unloaded, locked in hard-sided cases, stored in the trunk, ammunition separate. Do not stop for anything beyond fuel or emergencies. If New York is your destination, leave your firearms at home unless you possess a valid New York State license — and even then, consult a New York attorney about the current state of the rapidly evolving carry restrictions.***
