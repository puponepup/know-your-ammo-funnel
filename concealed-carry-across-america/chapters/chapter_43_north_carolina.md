**Chapter 43**

**North Carolina**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 70/100 — A traditionally gun-friendly state with strong self-defense rights and universal permit recognition, though the absence of permitless carry and some local-level restrictions temper the score.*

North Carolina's combination of Southern tradition, hospitality, and practical regulation makes it a relatively welcoming destination for lawful gun owners. The state recognizes concealed carry permits from every other state, maintains a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, allows open carry in most public areas, and permits vehicle carry of loaded handguns in plain view. However, North Carolina has not adopted permitless carry, issues concealed handgun permits to residents only, and maintains some location-based carry restrictions worth understanding before traveling there.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine guns require a sheriff's permit; other NFA items lawful with federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, but some carry restrictions at the local level remain possible
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas, but certain specified locations may be off-limits
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes concealed carry permits from all other states regardless of the holder's age
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be secured in holsters or slings and carried openly in the passenger compartment; concealed firearms should be placed in the trunk
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for permit holders; private schools may prohibit
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles; private schools may prohibit all firearms
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required immediately upon any official contact
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry if the premises are not posted against firearms
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Localities may prohibit carry in terminal areas; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

North Carolina issues concealed handgun permits to residents who are at least 21 years old. Permits are valid for five years and allow the concealed, loaded carry of handguns in most public areas. The state does not issue permits to non-residents but recognizes valid carry permits from all other states.

When interacting with law enforcement, permit holders must immediately disclose that they are carrying a handgun and possess a valid permit.

Certain areas remain off-limits, including state government buildings, the State Capitol, courthouses, all educational properties (including colleges and universities), the State Fairgrounds, and any location clearly posted to prohibit firearms. However, handguns may be lawfully stored in locked vehicles on the premises of government buildings, courthouses, educational institutions, and the State Fair. Concealed carry is also permitted while attending a church located on school property, provided the property is not posted against firearms.

**Without a Permit**

Vehicle carry of loaded handguns is restricted to firearms kept in plain view. A pistol placed in a hip holster, on the dashboard, or on the passenger seat is acceptable as long as it remains clearly visible from outside the vehicle. Courts have consistently ruled that any concealment of a readily accessible handgun may be interpreted as unlawful concealed carry. Since "concealment" includes any firearm "on or about the person," storing a handgun in a glove compartment or console box is not recommended. If the firearm is not visible, it should be stored in the trunk or rearmost area of the vehicle and secured in a gun case.

Open carry of a loaded handgun while on foot is legal in many public spaces. However, non-permittees are prohibited from carrying firearms at funeral processions, parades, educational institutions (including colleges and universities), establishments where alcohol is served, the State Capitol, and any state court facility.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

The state's preemption law generally prevents local governments from enacting their own firearm regulations, though limited local carry restrictions remain possible. All carriers should be attentive to posted signage, particularly in urban areas and at educational institutions.

> ***Key Takeaway: North Carolina is a welcoming state for armed travelers, particularly those who hold any valid concealed carry permit. Universal reciprocity means your permit will be honored here regardless of which state issued it. The main limitation is that vehicle carry of loaded handguns for non-permittees requires the firearm to remain in plain view — concealment in a glove box or console is risky. If you plan to carry on foot without a permit, open carry is broadly permitted but watch for location restrictions at schools, government buildings, and establishments serving alcohol.***
