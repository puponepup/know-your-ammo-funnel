**Chapter 44**

**North Dakota**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 80/100 — Constitutional carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, and broad vehicle carry rights. However, open carry is restricted to permit holders only, and permitless carriers must keep firearms concealed.*

North Dakota's harsh winters have shaped a resilient population that relies on firearms more frequently than most. The state adopted constitutional carry in 2019, and its combination of strong preemption, Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, and permissive vehicle carry laws make it a solidly gun-friendly destination. The one notable exception is that open carry of loaded firearms is restricted to individuals with recognized carry permits — permitless carriers must keep their firearms concealed.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, though local regulation of some carry at public gatherings is not fully preempted
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Restricted — loaded open carry prohibited for non-permittees (some exceptions)
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Class I and Class II permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 18 or older who meet Class 2 license qualifications may carry concealed
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states with reciprocal recognition; holder must reside in the issuing state
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful deadly weapon (not limited to handguns)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for anyone (permit holders and non-permit holders)
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permit holders may carry loaded handguns; some localities may enforce their own policies
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Upon demand for permit holders; immediately upon contact for permitless carriers
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms in vehicles
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted if the area is open to those under 21
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless church leaders grant explicit permission
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy; statutory exception allows loaded firearms in hotel rooms
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in most passenger terminals; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Licenses are issued by the Bureau of Criminal Investigation to qualified individuals aged 18 and older, with each license valid for five years. These licenses are categorized as Class I and Class II permits, both of which authorize open or concealed carry of any firearm — not limited to handguns. North Dakota issues permits to non-residents and recognizes permits from any state that offers reciprocal recognition, though the holder must reside in the state that issued the permit.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone who qualifies for a Class 2 license — meaning they are at least 18 years old, not a felon, and meet other basic requirements — and possesses a driver's license or state-issued ID from any U.S. state or territory may carry a concealed, loaded firearm without a permit, whether on foot or in a vehicle. The firearm must remain concealed, and the individual must immediately notify law enforcement upon official contact.

Those who do not qualify for permitless carry must transport firearms in a vehicle unloaded and secured in a commercial gun case or the trunk. To be considered unloaded, the chamber must be empty. Magazines may remain loaded, even if stored with the firearm.

On foot, carrying a loaded handgun is allowed when engaged in lawful hunting or target shooting. Loaded carry is also permitted at temporary residences such as campsites or hotel rooms.

Unlike many other states, North Dakota restricts open carry to individuals with recognized carry permits. Those carrying under the permitless carry provision must keep their firearms concealed.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are prohibited for everyone — including recognized permit holders — in bars, casinos, gaming sites, and "public gatherings." The term "public gatherings" includes schools and school-sponsored events, churches (unless church policy explicitly allows carry), and public buildings (with exceptions for highway rest areas and restrooms). However, keeping firearms secured in vehicles at these locations, as well as at universities and most workplaces, is lawful.

North Dakota's preemption law restricts cities from regulating most aspects of firearm possession and ownership, though limited exceptions allow local governments to impose certain carry restrictions at public gatherings.

> ***Key Takeaway: North Dakota is a solidly gun-friendly state with constitutional carry at 18 and exceptionally broad vehicle carry rights. The key distinction from many other permitless carry states is that open carry is restricted to permit holders only — those carrying without a permit must keep their firearms concealed. The duty to immediately notify law enforcement upon contact is mandatory for permitless carriers. The Class I permit is worth obtaining for its broad reciprocity and its unique allowance of any lawful deadly weapon rather than being limited to handguns only.***
