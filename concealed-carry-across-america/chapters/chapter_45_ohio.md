**Chapter 45**

**Ohio**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 82/100 — Permitless carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal out-of-state permit recognition, and solid preemption protections. Long gun transport rules and some location restrictions are the main caveats.*

Ohio's journey to strong carry rights culminated in 2022 with the adoption of permitless carry. The Buckeye State now allows any "qualifying adult" aged 21 or older to carry a loaded handgun — either concealed or openly — in most public spaces. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, universal out-of-state permit recognition, and statewide preemption of firearms laws, Ohio ranks among the more favorable states for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and becoming more commonly accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and some non-residents (those employed in Ohio); five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any "qualifying adult" aged 21 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes all non-resident carry permits
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Long guns must be unloaded and in plain view with actions open, or enclosed in cases; any qualifying adult may carry a loaded handgun
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for permit holders and qualifying adults
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Ohio permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit individuals from possessing firearms in their vehicles
- **State parks:** Carry prohibited in most buildings; carry permitted in standalone restrooms and outdoor areas
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless church leaders grant explicit permission
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities unless posted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Despite the adoption of permitless carry, Ohio continues to issue concealed handgun licenses. A sheriff from a person's home county or an adjacent county will grant a license to any qualified resident aged 21 or older, valid for five years. Non-residents employed in Ohio are also eligible. Ohio honors out-of-state permits held by non-residents, and residents with permits from states that have reciprocity agreements with Ohio are also recognized. Active duty military members with valid military IDs and proof of required training may carry as if they hold a permit.

**Without a Permit**

Any "qualifying adult" who is 21 years of age or older, legally allowed to possess a firearm, otherwise eligible for a license, and lawfully present in the United States may carry a loaded handgun — either concealed or openly — in most public spaces. A qualifying adult has the same rights and limitations as a licensed permit holder and will forfeit carry rights if they no longer meet the legal criteria required of a licensee.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Permit holders, qualifying adults, and military personnel may not carry firearms in police stations or jails, colleges (unless specifically authorized), K-12 schools, bars (unless not consuming alcohol), churches (unless permission is granted), mental health facilities, courthouses, government buildings used for official business, and any private businesses that are properly posted. State and local government buildings primarily serving as restrooms, rest areas, or parking facilities are excepted.

Parking lots of most restricted locations are legal for carry, provided the handgun remains in the vehicle and is secured in a locked compartment when the vehicle is unattended.

An important exception applies to K-12 school zones: under federal law, carrying a loaded handgun in a vehicle within a school zone requires a valid in-state license. Individuals without an Ohio-issued license may not meet this requirement.

Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded and placed in secured gun cases while in a vehicle. Loaded magazines may be stored in the trunk or another container that provides a separate enclosure. A firearm and magazine may be stored in the same container as long as the magazine is kept in a distinct, snapped compartment within that container.

During a traffic stop, individuals must stay in their vehicle, keep their hands visible at all times, and must not reach for or touch the handgun. They must inform the officer they are carrying a firearm when asked. Firearms in a vehicle must be kept inaccessible to children or anyone legally prohibited from possessing a firearm.

> ***Key Takeaway: Ohio is a strong state for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 21, universal out-of-state permit recognition, stand-your-ground protections, and statewide preemption create a favorable environment. The primary nuances to remember are the long gun transport rules (unloaded, cased, or in plain view with actions open), the K-12 school zone requirement for an in-state license, and the traffic stop protocol. Obtaining an Ohio license — or maintaining your home-state license — remains valuable for school zone compliance and interstate reciprocity.***
