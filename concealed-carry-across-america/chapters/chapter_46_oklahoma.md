**Chapter 46**

**Oklahoma**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — Constitutional carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal permit recognition, statewide preemption, and broad vehicle carry rights. The .45 caliber limitation is the main quirk.*

Oklahoma's frontier heritage runs deep, and the state embraced constitutional carry in 2019. Combined with universal recognition of all out-of-state permits, a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, strong preemption, and permissive vehicle carry laws, the Sooner State ranks among the most favorable environments for armed travelers in the country. The most notable quirk is a .45 caliber maximum for handguns carried under a permit.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No restrictions; carry limited to ammunition that is .45 caliber or less
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state; civil suits allowed against violating localities
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas under constitutional carry
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; 5- or 10-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any law-abiding individual 21 or older (18-20 for military members/veterans)
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes carry permits from all other states; military IDs also recognized
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns .45 caliber or less
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; presence must be disclosed when demanded by law enforcement
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner (vehicle only)
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Oklahoma permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms in vehicles
- **State parks:** Carry permitted in open areas but not in government buildings on park grounds
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining (no alcohol consumption while carrying)
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in government-owned terminals; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry prohibited in buildings; carry permitted outdoors and in parking areas

**For Permit Holders**

Oklahoma continues to issue licenses for residents aged 21 and older (or 18-20 if actively serving in the armed forces) to carry loaded handguns, either on foot or in a vehicle. Licenses are valid for 5 or 10 years. The state recognizes all out-of-state carry permits and military IDs for armed forces members.

Recognized permit holders may openly or concealed carry holstered handguns (.45 caliber or less) in most public areas, except for locations listed in the universal restrictions section.

**Without a Permit**

Any law-abiding individual aged 21 or older (or 18-20 if they are a military member or veteran) may carry a loaded firearm — handgun, rifle, or shotgun — openly or concealed without a permit in most public areas. Firearms may also be carried in vehicles or boats. During a traffic stop, individuals must disclose the presence of a firearm when requested by an officer.

Those exercising the permitless carry option must not have prior convictions related to illegal drug use, domestic abuse, stalking, or aggravated assault. A recent change in law permits anyone to carry firearms in restaurants serving alcohol, as long as they do not consume alcohol while carrying.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Carry is prohibited for everyone in bars, sports arenas, casinos, K-12 schools, colleges and universities, technology centers, most government-owned buildings (including those at rest areas, state parks, and airports), and private businesses that have posted restrictions. Outdoor events with controlled entry points featuring metal detectors may also prohibit carry; if no metal detectors are in place, concealed handgun carry is permitted.

Vehicle possession of firearms in parking lots at restricted areas is lawful. Carry is allowed in wildlife refuges, parks, fairgrounds, and recreational areas, as long as it does not occur within buildings on those premises. In municipal zoos or parks, carry is limited to a concealed handgun.

Oklahoma law prevents property owners from enforcing policies that restrict firearms and ammunition in vehicles parked on their property. Entering posted private businesses without permission carries no criminal penalty unless the individual refuses to leave. The state's preemption law prevents most local regulation of carry rights and allows civil suits against localities that violate its provisions.

> ***Key Takeaway: Oklahoma is one of the most accommodating states for armed travelers. Constitutional carry at 21 (18 for military), universal permit recognition, vehicle carry with no concealment restrictions, strong preemption, and parking lot protections make this state exceptionally permissive. The .45 caliber limitation for permitted carry is the main quirk to remember — if you carry a 10mm or similar larger-caliber handgun, be aware of this restriction. Restaurant carry is permitted even without a license, provided you abstain from alcohol. The building-level restrictions at government facilities (rest areas, state parks, airports) are worth noting, but outdoor and parking lot carry at those locations remains lawful.***
