**Chapter 47**

**Oregon**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 30/100 — A state trending sharply toward restriction. No out-of-state permit recognition, a magazine capacity ban (currently under legal challenge), permit-to-purchase requirements, and local regulation of loaded carry make Oregon increasingly difficult for armed travelers.*

Oregon's political landscape has shifted its firearms laws decisively to the left. The state does not recognize concealed carry permits from any other state, requires a permit to purchase or transfer firearms, has banned the import and most possession of magazines holding more than 10 rounds, and allows local governments to regulate the public carry of loaded firearms. Portland and several other municipalities have enacted local ordinances requiring firearms carried in public or in vehicles to be unloaded and cased unless the individual holds an Oregon license. While open carry remains technically lawful at the state level, the practical reality is far more restrictive.

- **Open carry:** Lawful under state law in most public areas, but local restrictions may apply; "public buildings" off-limits to non-permittees

- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No out-of-state permits recognized

- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms must be carried in plain view or securely encased in the trunk or storage compartment; local restrictions may apply

**For Permit Holders**

Oregon requires a license to carry a concealed handgun, whether on one's person or in a vehicle. Permits are issued by the sheriff of the applicant's home county to qualified individuals aged 21 or older, valid for four-year terms. Oregon grants licenses to non-residents only if they live in neighboring states and can demonstrate a valid need for concealed carry within Oregon. These permits are issued at the discretion of local sheriffs.

Oregon does not honor concealed carry permits from any other state. Those with an Oregon permit may carry in most public areas, excluding schools and colleges (if posted), the State Capitol, and major airport terminals.

**Without an Oregon Permit**

A loaded handgun may be carried in a vehicle if it is secured in a visible belt holster. However, any handgun that is easily accessible and hidden from view — such as those placed in unlocked glove compartments, console boxes, or under a seat — is prohibited. Handguns stored in secured cases within a vehicle's trunk are permitted. In vehicles without a trunk, a handgun may be kept in a locked glove compartment or console box, provided the key is not inserted.

For motorcycles, ATVs, or snowmobiles, a concealed handgun must be either trigger-locked or stored in a locked container. Long guns may be loaded and transported if placed in closed containers or stored in a trunk.

RV owners may lawfully possess a loaded, concealed handgun while the RV is parked at a campsite and used as a residence. Standard vehicle carry laws apply when the RV is in motion.

Oregon's preemption law allows local governments to regulate the public carry of loaded firearms. Cities like Portland require that guns carried in public or in private vehicles be unloaded and either plainly visible or securely cased unless the individual holds an Oregon license. State law also prohibits open carry by non-permit holders in any "public building," which includes K-12 schools, colleges, hospitals, the State Capitol, government offices, major airport terminals, city halls, rest areas, and the homes of elected state officials.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Oregon law requires that any unattended firearm be both trigger-locked and secured in a locked container when not in the owner's possession. If the unattended firearm is a handgun left in an unoccupied vehicle, it must also be concealed from view. The state further prohibits all firearm possession within court facilities and at racecourses.

Oregon also bans the import and most possession of magazines holding more than 10 rounds. Limited possession is allowed for residents who owned such magazines prior to December 22, 2022. Visitors are strictly prohibited from importing them. This law is currently under legal challenge and may be modified.

> ***Key Takeaway: Oregon is an increasingly difficult state for armed travelers. No out-of-state permits are recognized, meaning non-residents cannot legally carry concealed without a difficult-to-obtain Oregon non-resident permit (available only to residents of neighboring states with demonstrated need). The magazine capacity ban prohibits bringing standard-capacity magazines into the state. Local regulations — particularly in Portland — add another layer of restriction beyond state law. If your travel requires passing through Oregon without an Oregon permit, keep all firearms unloaded, cased, and stored securely. Check local ordinances before stopping in any municipality, as carry rules may vary city to city.***
