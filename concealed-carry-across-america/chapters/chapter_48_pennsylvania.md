**Chapter 48**

**Pennsylvania**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 62/100 — A state of contrasts: strong Castle Doctrine, preemption, and open carry coexist with a license requirement for vehicle carry and concealed carry. Philadelphia has stricter rules than the rest of the state.*

Pennsylvania blends both urban and rural characteristics — famously described as "Philadelphia on one side, Pittsburgh on the other, and Alabama in the middle." Gun ownership is broadly valued, and the state features a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, statewide preemption, and open carry in most public areas. However, a license is required for concealed carry and for carrying a loaded handgun in a vehicle, and Philadelphia imposes additional restrictions that don't apply elsewhere in the state.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas (exceptions include Philadelphia, where a license is required)
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic recognition for vehicle carry of handguns by non-residents with out-of-state permits; conditional recognition for on-foot carry
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and certain NFA-registered short-barreled shotguns and rifles
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handguns must be unloaded and securely locked in the trunk or rear storage area; rifles and shotguns must be unloaded and may be in the passenger compartment but should be cased
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearm possession prohibited; legal defenses exist
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Pennsylvania requires a license to carry a loaded handgun in a vehicle or concealed on one's person. Permits are issued by the sheriff of a resident's home county to individuals 21 years or older, with licenses valid for five years. Non-residents may also obtain a license from any sheriff's office if they hold a permit from their home state, though recent reports suggest only certain sheriffs accept non-resident applications.

Non-residents with a valid out-of-state permit are permitted to carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle, provided it is kept within the vehicle's interior. To carry concealed outside the vehicle, a license recognized by the Attorney General is required.

Permit holders may carry in most public areas, with exceptions including K-12 schools, courthouses (where posting is required), the State Capitol, and mental health facilities.

**Without a Permit**

Vehicle carry of any firearm by an unlicensed individual is strictly regulated. Handguns must be unloaded, cased, and secured in the trunk or rear storage area, with the firearm separated from ammunition. Travelers may only transport firearms in this manner if they are en route to a vacation dwelling, gun range, or residence.

Open carry of a loaded handgun while on foot is permitted in most public areas, except those restricted for permit holders. The handgun must be in a visible holster, and the carrier may not enter a vehicle with the firearm. Courts have ruled that "absolute invisibility" is not necessary for a firearm to be considered "concealed," so caution is advised.

The preemption law prevents most localities from regulating open carry, and law enforcement is generally familiar with its legal status. However, in Philadelphia, handgun carry is limited to those possessing valid, recognized licenses — open carry without a license is effectively prohibited there.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded and secured in commercial cases or gun racks. Long guns may be transported in the passenger compartment but must remain unloaded, regardless of whether the individual holds a carry permit. Licensed firearm carry is limited to handguns and certain short-barreled shotguns and rifles registered under the National Firearms Act.

> ***Key Takeaway: Pennsylvania is workable for armed travelers who hold a recognized permit. Non-residents with out-of-state permits can carry a loaded handgun in their vehicle throughout the state, but on-foot concealed carry requires an Attorney General-recognized license. Open carry on foot is legal in most of the state but completely off-limits in Philadelphia without a license. The vehicle carry restriction for unlicensed individuals — unloaded, cased, trunk-stored, ammunition separated — is strict and must be followed precisely. If your route takes you through Philadelphia, ensure you have a recognized permit before carrying in any manner.***
