**Chapter 49**

**Rhode Island**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 15/100 — Restrictive Northeast carry laws, no out-of-state permit recognition for on-foot carry, machine gun and suppressor ownership prohibited, magazine capacity limited to 10 rounds, and mandatory "safe storage" of all unattended firearms.*

Rhode Island reflects the restrictive stance common along much of the Northeast coast. Carry is only permitted for those with a Rhode Island permit, and travelers from other states must forfeit their on-foot carry rights upon crossing the state line. The state prohibits machine guns and suppressors, bans magazines exceeding 10 rounds (with no grandfather clause), requires mandatory safe storage of all unattended firearms, imposes a duty to retreat in public, and permits hotels to eject guests for firearm possession.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No permit required for possession; ammunition and pistol sales require a "blue card" (safety certificate)
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Restricted; magazines exceeding 10 rounds prohibited (no grandfather clause)
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Personal ownership prohibited; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public areas
- **Open carry:** Prohibited in all public areas unless the individual holds an Attorney General-issued permit
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents by local police or the Attorney General
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** No general recognition; limited recognition for vehicle-only transit through the state (see details below)
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** A carry permit issued by any state or locality is required for handgun transport; otherwise McClure-Volkmer protocols apply; unloaded rifles and shotguns may be transported without a permit
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearms must be unloaded and in locked containers
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Firearms carry prohibited; firearms must be unloaded and cased in vehicles
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Hotels may reject or eject guests for firearm possession by statute
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Rhode Island permit holder carry permitted in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Rhode Island requires a license to carry a handgun on one's person or in a vehicle. Residents can apply through their local police chief, while non-residents holding a carry permit from another state can apply to any city's police chief. The Attorney General also issues permits to non-residents without requiring a permit from another state. Licenses are valid for four years.

Locally issued permits require concealed carry, whereas Attorney General-issued permits allow open carry.

Applicants must be at least 21 years old and demonstrate proficiency in firearms handling. Permit holders can carry in most public areas, excluding K-12 schools, state parks, and forests.

Rhode Island recognizes carry permits issued by other states or localities for vehicle transit only: the handgun must remain within the vehicle's interior, and the individual must be passing through the state on a continuous journey with no intent to stay. Even a one-day stay would not qualify for this exception.

**Without a Rhode Island Permit**

A handgun can only be transported in a vehicle if it is unloaded, secured in a gun case, and the ammunition is stored in the trunk or an external storage compartment. If the vehicle does not have a trunk, ammunition must be kept in a separate locked container. The individual must be traveling between their home and business, or en route to a gun shop, gunsmith, repair facility, or target range — unless they qualify as a traveler on a "through journey" under federal protections.

Unloaded rifles and shotguns may be transported in a vehicle with loaded magazines, provided the magazines are not attached to the firearms.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Rhode Island mandates that any "unattended" firearm — including handguns — be secured in a locked container or made inoperable with a trigger lock. This rule applies when the owner is not in "close proximity" to the firearm. Commercial gun cases are recommended for compliance.

Gun possession is prohibited on K-12 school grounds, with exceptions for law enforcement and individuals who secure their unloaded firearms in locked containers within their vehicles.

The state has banned the import and possession of magazines holding more than 10 rounds. No grandfather clause was included, meaning even magazines possessed prior to the law's passage are now prohibited.

> ***Key Takeaway: Rhode Island is a challenging state for armed travelers. Out-of-state permits are recognized only for continuous vehicle transit through the state — any stops or overnight stays require a Rhode Island-issued permit. Machine guns and suppressors are prohibited, magazines are capped at 10 rounds with no grandfather clause, and mandatory safe storage applies to all unattended firearms. Hotels may legally eject guests for possessing firearms. If you must pass through Rhode Island, keep firearms unloaded, cased, and locked, with ammunition stored separately. If your destination is Rhode Island, either obtain a state-issued permit or leave your firearms at home.***
