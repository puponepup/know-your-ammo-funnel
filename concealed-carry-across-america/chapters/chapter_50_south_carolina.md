**Chapter 50**

**South Carolina**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — Permitless carry at 18, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, broad vehicle carry rights, and open carry protected by statute. A top-tier destination for armed travelers.*

The adoption of permitless carry has made South Carolina a premier destination for traveling gun owners. Anyone 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm can carry a loaded handgun — openly or concealed — in most public areas without a permit. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, and statutory protection for open carry (a person carrying a handgun in a snapped holster cannot be searched, detained, or arrested on that basis alone), the Palmetto State provides one of the most favorable legal environments in the country.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, but localities may regulate open carry at certain outdoor public events
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas; protected by statute
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and some qualified non-residents (property owners, military); no cost; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states that require a background check and gun safety training
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; rifles and shotguns must be unloaded when visiting certain areas
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Loaded handguns lawful; must be stored in a closed compartment
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Permit holders may carry loaded handguns in a closed glove compartment, console box, or trunk
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Handgun carry by any law-abiding adult 18 or older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Anyone may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited unless church leaders grant express permission
- **Hotels:** Hotels may reject or eject guests and force-of-law posting prohibitions apply
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited if the terminal is publicly owned; vehicle carry permitted for permit holders
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

South Carolina issues licenses to carry a handgun on or about one's person. Residents who are at least 18 years old can obtain a permit at no cost, valid for five years. Qualified non-residents who own real estate in the state are also eligible.

Licenses from other states are honored, provided those states require a criminal background check and gun safety training for permit holders. The out-of-state permit holder must reside in the issuing state and may carry a handgun either openly or concealed.

In a vehicle, this includes carrying the firearm on one's person or anywhere inside the vehicle. On foot, carrying is permitted in most public areas except those listed in the universal restrictions section.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry a loaded handgun, either openly or concealed. In a vehicle, this includes any location within the passenger compartment or trunk. On foot, carrying is permitted in most public areas with the exception of restricted locations.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Carry is prohibited in police stations, jails, courthouses, polling places on election days, scholastic athletic events unrelated to firearms, medical facilities, preschools and daycares, local government meetings and most government offices, churches (without express owner permission), public transportation, and any private businesses or special events that post signs banning firearms. Local governments may also restrict open carry at permitted outdoor events such as parades and fairs. Entry into a private residence requires the express consent of the homeowner.

Vehicle carry of loaded rifles and shotguns is generally legal, except when traveling to state parks, recreational areas, or wildlife areas during hunting season — in which case long guns must be unloaded and stored in cases. Schools, universities, and the State Capitol are off-limits to firearms unless the handgun is secured in a closed compartment of a locked vehicle.

Firearm possession is banned inside publicly owned buildings (except rest areas), though a recent Attorney General opinion clarified that carry outside one's vehicle in parking lots or garages of publicly owned buildings — excluding schools and colleges — is lawful.

Open carry is protected by statute. A person carrying a handgun in a snapped holster does not give law enforcement probable cause for search, detention, or arrest.

> ***Key Takeaway: South Carolina is one of the strongest states in the country for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, statutory protection for open carry, unrestricted vehicle carry of loaded handguns, and permissive restaurant carry create an exceptionally favorable environment. The main areas to watch are churches (express permission required), publicly owned buildings, and the handful of location-specific restrictions. The free, no-cost permit is worth obtaining for enhanced reciprocity when traveling to other states.***
