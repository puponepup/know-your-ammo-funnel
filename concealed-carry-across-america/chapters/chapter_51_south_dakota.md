**Chapter 51**

**South Dakota**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 88/100 — Permitless carry at 18, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal permit recognition, broad vehicle carry, and a preemption law with teeth. Enhanced permits offer additional privileges at schools and the State Capitol.*

South Dakota adopted permitless carry in 2019, building on its long tradition of firearms culture. The state allows any individual 18 or older who can lawfully possess a firearm to carry concealed without a permit. Open carry is broadly accepted, vehicle carry of loaded firearms — including long guns — is unrestricted, and the preemption law now includes civil penalties and Attorney General enforcement against violating localities. The enhanced permit tier offers additional privileges, including carry in the State Capitol and public K-12 schools with authorization.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law with civil penalties and Attorney General prosecution for local violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard and enhanced permits issued to residents only; five-year terms; all fees eliminated
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes all out-of-state carry permits
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms — including rifles and shotguns — may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Firearms prohibited at public schools; permitted at nonpublic schools
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

South Dakota issues licenses for carrying concealed handguns, either on one's person or in a vehicle. Standard permits are available through county sheriffs to residents aged 18 and older. The state also offers an enhanced permit for residents aged 21 and older, which provides broader reciprocity with other states. All permit fees have been eliminated.

Enhanced permit holders enjoy additional privileges: they may carry concealed handguns in the State Capitol if they notify the Highway Patrol at least 24 hours in advance, and may carry in public K-12 schools with written authorization from school officials.

**Without a Permit**

No license is required to carry a concealed, loaded handgun either on foot or in a vehicle. Open carry of a loaded handgun is also permitted under most circumstances and is especially common in rural areas.

Loaded rifles and shotguns may be carried anywhere within a vehicle. When traveling through game preserves or wildlife refuges, long guns should be placed in cases. Riders on motorcycles or snowmobiles are also permitted to carry loaded firearms without a permit.

The term "loaded" refers to having a round in the chamber (including any chamber of a revolver cylinder); a firearm is not considered loaded simply because a magazine contains ammunition.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearms are generally prohibited in K-12 public schools, bars, courthouses, and the State Capitol. Carry is permitted at nonpublic K-12 schools for permit holders in accordance with federal law.

South Dakota's preemption law prohibits local governments from regulating most aspects of firearm carry, possession, and transport. Recent updates allow for civil penalties and enforcement actions by the Attorney General against any locality that violates these protections.

> ***Key Takeaway: South Dakota is one of the most permissive states in the country for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, unrestricted vehicle carry of loaded firearms (including long guns), universal out-of-state permit recognition, and a preemption law with enforcement teeth create an exceptionally favorable environment. The enhanced permit is worth obtaining for its additional privileges at the State Capitol and K-12 schools, as well as for maximizing reciprocity when traveling to other states. The main restrictions are limited to bars, courthouses, schools, and the Capitol.***
