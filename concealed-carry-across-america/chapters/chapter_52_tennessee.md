**Chapter 52**

**Tennessee**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 82/100 — Permitless carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal permit recognition, and broad vehicle carry rights. DUI conviction disqualifiers and some "open area" restrictions for permitless carriers are the main nuances.*

Tennessee has long been a welcoming state for gun owners, and the adoption of permitless carry further solidified its position. The state allows anyone 18 or older (established by court ruling) who may lawfully possess a handgun to carry openly or concealed without a permit, subject to certain qualifying conditions. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, universal out-of-state permit recognition, and statewide preemption, Tennessee offers a strong legal environment for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform statewide; localities may be sued for violations
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful for permit holders and those qualifying for permitless carry
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Enhanced permits (8-year or lifetime) and concealed-only permits (5-year) issued to residents; non-residents eligible if regularly employed in the state
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 18 or older who meet qualifying criteria (see details)
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes all non-resident carry permits
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms — including rifles, shotguns, and handguns — may be carried anywhere in a vehicle or boat
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner; code enforcement is preempted
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Tennessee permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms in vehicles
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Open or concealed carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Hotels may reject or eject guests; force-of-law posting prohibitions apply
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities unless posted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on grounds unless posted

**For Permit Holders**

Tennessee continues to issue licenses for carrying loaded handguns, either openly or concealed. Enhanced handgun carry permits are issued to residents aged 18 or older (per court ruling) and are valid for either 8 years or for life. The state also offers a "concealed carry only" permit, valid for 5 years, which requires less training but comes with more restrictions on where and how the firearm can be carried.

Tennessee honors all out-of-state carry permits, provided the holder is not a Tennessee resident and limits carry to handguns. Non-residents may obtain an 8-year permit only if they are regularly employed within the state.

Certain areas are typically posted to prohibit carry, including schools, colleges, civic centers, and public recreational buildings. In some locations, signage may either ban all firearms or permit only concealed carry by licensees.

**Without a Permit**

Any individual aged 18 or older may carry a loaded handgun — either openly or concealed — in most public areas under Tennessee's permitless carry law. However, permitless carriers are restricted from entering locations that are off-limits to permit holders. Additionally, certain "open areas" including public parks, campgrounds, and nature trails remain off-limits without a permit.

Permitless carriers may also be barred from buildings that permit only concealed carry by licensed individuals but prohibit all other forms of carry. Individuals with certain convictions are disqualified from carrying without a permit — this includes anyone convicted of stalking (permanently) and those with DUI convictions (once within 5 years or more than once in 10 years).

The carry of loaded firearms — including rifles, shotguns, and handguns — is permitted in motor vehicles and boats. Firearms may be stored in the glove box, console, trunk, under the seat, or in secured gun cases.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Tennessee's preemption law prevents local governments from enacting their own firearms regulations and allows civil suits against localities that violate its provisions. Pepper spray and stun gun possession are allowed at schools and colleges.

Posted signage must be observed. Locations that post restrictions on carry can apply those restrictions to all property owned by the posting entity. "Open areas" like parks and campgrounds generally allow carry unless a school-sponsored event is taking place nearby.

> ***Key Takeaway: Tennessee is a strong state for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 18, universal permit recognition, unrestricted vehicle carry (including long guns), stand-your-ground protections, and statewide preemption create a favorable environment. The key nuances are the DUI disqualifier for permitless carry, the "open area" restrictions that affect only permitless carriers (parks and campgrounds require a permit), and posted signage that must be observed. The enhanced permit is worth obtaining for access to these additional locations and for maximizing interstate reciprocity.***
