**Chapter 53**

**Texas**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 88/100 — The Lone Star State lives up to its reputation. Constitutional carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, broad vehicle carry, hotel protections for gun owners, and a deeply rooted firearms culture.*

Texas has long been synonymous with gun ownership, and the adoption of constitutional carry cemented the state's position among the most favorable environments for armed travelers. The state allows anyone 18 or older (per court ruling) to carry a loaded handgun — openly in a holster or concealed — without a permit. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, preemption, and a law that prohibits hotels from barring firearm possession by guests, Texas delivers on its reputation.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption statute in effect, but cities may regulate long gun carry in certain areas
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas provided the handgun is holstered
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; initial 4-year term, 5-year renewals
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 18 or older (per court ruling) who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — honors permits from states requiring background checks for permit holders
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Concealed, loaded handguns may be carried anywhere; visible handguns must be holstered; loaded long guns (concealed or visible) may be carried anywhere
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for permit holders and their firearms
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Texas permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required immediately upon official contact (identification request)
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted per administrative code
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Hotels are prohibited from barring firearm possession by guests; may require firearms remain concealed
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas unless posted; vehicle carry in parking facilities permitted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Although Texas no longer requires a permit to carry a handgun, the state still issues licenses to residents who are at least 18 years old (per court ruling), with an initial term of 4 years and a 5-year renewal period. Non-residents are also eligible for permits.

Texas honors licenses from states that require background checks for permit holders. Permit holders must be at least 18, carry handguns either concealed or — if openly — in a holster, and present their license when asked for identification by law enforcement.

Carry is prohibited in specific locations including courthouses, polling places, racetracks, K-12 schools (including events on school property), correctional and civil commitment facilities, mental hospitals, and sporting events. Colleges allow concealed carry with restrictions. General hospitals, nursing homes, amusement parks, bars, and collegiate athletic events are off-limits if appropriately posted.

Handguns secured in vehicles in parking lots of restricted areas remain lawful. Any business or church may prohibit both open and concealed carry by posting proper signage.

**Without a Permit**

Anyone 18 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry a loaded handgun, either openly (in a holster) or concealed, in most public areas. In a vehicle, this includes any location within the passenger compartment or trunk. On foot, carrying is permitted in most public areas except those listed in the universal restrictions section.

The carry of loaded rifles, shotguns, and handguns is permitted in motor vehicles and boats. Firearms may be stored in the glove box, console, trunk, under the seat, or in secured gun cases.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Carry is prohibited in police stations, jails, courthouses, polling places on election days, scholastic athletic events, medical facilities, preschools and daycares, local government meetings and most offices, churches (without owner permission), public transportation, and any private businesses or special events that post signs banning carry. Local governments may also restrict open carry at permitted outdoor events.

Vehicle carry of loaded rifles and shotguns is generally legal, except when traveling to state parks, recreational areas, or wildlife areas during hunting season — in which case long guns must be unloaded and stored in cases. Schools, universities, and the State Capitol are off-limits unless the handgun is secured in a closed compartment of a locked vehicle.

Open carry is protected by statute. A person carrying a handgun in a snapped holster does not give law enforcement probable cause for search, detention, or arrest.

> ***Key Takeaway: Texas fully delivers on its reputation as a gun-friendly state. Constitutional carry at 18, broad vehicle carry of all firearm types, hotel protections for armed guests, stand-your-ground protections, and a deeply rooted cultural acceptance of firearms make this one of the best states for armed travelers. The main items to remember are the holster requirement for open carry, the posted signage rules for businesses and churches, and the immediate duty to notify law enforcement upon contact. The Texas License to Carry remains valuable for interstate reciprocity and for bypassing some posted restrictions.***
