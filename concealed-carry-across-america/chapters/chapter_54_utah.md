**Chapter 54**

**Utah**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 85/100 — Constitutional carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal permit recognition, and one of the most sought-after non-resident permits in the country. Permit holders may carry any lawful firearm — not limited to handguns.*

Utah's firearms culture is deeply rooted in its pioneer heritage. The state adopted constitutional carry and continues to issue one of the most widely recognized non-resident permits in the country. Utah honors all out-of-state permits, features a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground protections, and — uniquely — allows permit holders to carry any lawful firearm, not just handguns. The state's preemption statute includes civil penalties for non-compliant localities.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption statute with civil penalties for non-compliance
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas for permit holders and those 21 and older
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms; provisional permits available for ages 18-20
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — individuals 21 or older who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes carry permits from all other states
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Any lawful firearm (not limited to handguns)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle; rifles and shotguns must be unloaded (chamber empty, magazines may contain ammunition)
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Lawful for any gun owner; some code enforcement possible
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Utah permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Most parking areas permitted; churches and government buildings may restrict
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by permit holders and those 21 and older permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited if "proper notice" (signs, direct communication, or public announcements) is provided by the church
- **Hotels:** Hotels may reject or eject guests for firearm possession by statute
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Utah issues permits for carrying loaded firearms — whether on foot or in a vehicle — that allow both open and concealed carry of any firearm, not just handguns. Permits are granted by the Bureau of Criminal Identification to individuals 21 or older and are valid for five years. Provisional licenses are available for those between 18 and 20. Both residents and non-residents can obtain permits.

Utah recognizes any valid out-of-state permit. Permit holders may carry in most public places, except in "secure areas" like courthouses, police stations, and other clearly posted locations. Private residences and churches can restrict carry by providing notice through signs, direct communication, or public announcements.

Resident permit holders aged 21 and older may carry firearms in K-12 schools and on college campuses, though some universities may enforce their own institutional rules.

**Without a Permit**

Individuals 21 or older may carry a loaded firearm, either openly or concealed, in most public areas. Those without permits are prohibited from carrying on K-12 or college campuses or inside preschools or nursery school buildings. However, vehicle carry is allowed in these areas unless it violates federal Gun-Free Zone laws.

Loaded handguns can be carried anywhere in a vehicle — under the seat, in the glove compartment, or within the console box — as long as the carrier either owns the vehicle or has the owner's consent and is at least 18 years old.

Long guns in a vehicle must remain unloaded, with firing chambers containing no live rounds, though their magazines may contain ammunition. Muzzle-loading firearms are considered loaded when capped or primed.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Most property owners are not allowed to enforce "no guns in vehicles" policies in their parking lots, as long as firearms are locked in the vehicle and secured from view. Government entities, churches, and single-family homes are exempt from this parking lot protection.

Utah's preemption law prevents local governments from regulating carry issues, ensuring consistent firearms laws statewide.

> ***Key Takeaway: Utah is an excellent state for armed travelers. Constitutional carry at 21, universal out-of-state permit recognition, the ability for permit holders to carry any lawful firearm (not just handguns), and strong preemption with enforcement create a highly favorable environment. The Utah non-resident permit is one of the most sought-after in the country due to its broad reciprocity — even travelers who live in restrictive states often obtain one. The long gun transport rule (unloaded in vehicles) and the church notice restriction are the main nuances to remember. Vehicle carry of loaded handguns is unrestricted for anyone 18 or older.***
