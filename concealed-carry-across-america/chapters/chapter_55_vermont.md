**Chapter 55**

**Vermont**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 68/100 — The original constitutional carry state — no permits have ever been required or issued. However, recent magazine capacity restrictions, bump stock bans, a duty to retreat, and safe storage requirements signal a leftward shift.*

Vermont holds the distinction of being the original constitutional carry state. No permits have ever been required or issued — any individual who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry it, openly or concealed, in most public areas. However, recent legislative changes have introduced magazine capacity limits, bump stock bans, and safe storage requirements, indicating that the state's historically unrestricted firearms environment is beginning to erode under shifting demographics and political pressures.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** Restricted; long gun magazines exceeding 10 rounds and handgun magazines exceeding 15 rounds are prohibited (some exceptions for pre-2018 possession, competition use, and .22 rimfire tubular magazines)
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption statute in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state (cities may regulate discharge)
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; duty to retreat applies in public places
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and generally accepted
- **Concealed carry licensing:** No licenses issued — permits are not required to carry
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any non-prohibited individual (the original constitutional carry state)
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Not applicable — no permit required to carry in Vermont
- **Weapons allowed:** Not applicable — no licensing system exists
- **Vehicle carry:** Rifles and shotguns must be unloaded; loaded handguns may be carried concealed anywhere in the vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Unloaded firearms in locked cases are lawful; loaded handgun carry would require a Vermont-issued permit, but since Vermont does not issue permits, loaded carry is effectively prohibited under the federal Gun-Free School Zones Act
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Not applicable — no permits issued by the state
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by any non-felon permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Any non-felon may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to hotel policy and force-of-law posting prohibitions
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Concealed carry permitted in buildings and on grounds

**Carrying in Vermont**

No license is required to carry a firearm in Vermont. Individuals may carry loaded handguns — either openly or concealed — in most public areas. Vehicle carry follows the same principle: a loaded, concealed handgun may be kept in the glove compartment, console, briefcase, or carried on one's person. The only requirement is that the carry is not undertaken with the intent to harm another person.

Rifles and shotguns may be transported openly or in commercial cases but must remain unloaded under conservation laws.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Firearm carry is prohibited in courthouses, school buildings, hospitals (if posted), polling places (if posted), and on the grounds of any "state institution" — which includes prisons, mental hospitals, and most state-owned or leased buildings managed by the Department of Buildings (excluding universities). State historic sites and highway rest areas permit only concealed carry.

Vermont bans possession of high-capacity magazines — defined as those holding more than 10 rounds for long guns or more than 15 rounds for handguns — as well as bump stocks. Magazines lawfully possessed by residents before April 11, 2018, are exempt, as are those used by non-residents in official shooting competitions. Attached tubular magazines for .22 rimfire firearms are also allowed. Magazines not qualifying for these exemptions may not be possessed, sold, transferred, or imported.

A recently passed safe storage law requires travelers to store firearms securely in RVs or vehicles to prevent child access.

> ***Key Takeaway: Vermont remains unique as the original constitutional carry state — no permits have ever been required or issued, and anyone who may lawfully possess a firearm may carry openly or concealed. However, recent restrictions have introduced complexity: magazine capacity limits (10 rounds for long guns, 15 for handguns), bump stock bans, a duty to retreat, and safe storage requirements. Critically, since Vermont does not issue any carry permits, residents effectively cannot carry loaded handguns in K-12 school zones under federal law. Travelers should leave magazines exceeding the capacity limits at home and be aware that the state's firearms environment, while still relatively permissive, is trending more restrictive.***
