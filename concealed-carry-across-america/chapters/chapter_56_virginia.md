**Chapter 56**

**Virginia**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 55/100 — A battleground state. Universal out-of-state permit recognition and open carry coexist with no Castle Doctrine, no preemption, local carry restrictions, and a loaded long gun magazine capacity limit in certain urban areas.*

Virginia has long held a strong commitment to gun rights, but recent demographic shifts have positioned the state as a key battleground in the ongoing fight over the Second Amendment. The state recognizes all out-of-state concealed carry permits, allows open carry in most public areas, and permits vehicle carry of loaded handguns in plain view or in compartments. However, the absence of a Castle Doctrine, no statewide preemption (localities may regulate carry in many public areas), and a 20-round magazine limit for non-permittees in certain urban areas create a more complex environment than many Southern states.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** "Streetsweeper"-style shotguns prohibited; magazines exceeding 20 rounds prohibited for non-permittees in certain urban public areas
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Machine guns require state registration; suppressors lawful with federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** No preemption — localities may regulate carry in many public areas; some counties may restrict loaded long guns in vehicles
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; stand-your-ground not codified in statute
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas, but localities may prohibit carry in some locations
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes carry permits from all other states (permittee must be at least 21 with government photo ID)
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried in plain view or secured in the glove compartment or console box; rifles and shotguns may be kept in gun cases in the rear of the passenger compartment or trunk
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** College regulation through Virginia's administrative code
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Virginia permit holders may carry loaded handguns; firearms should be unloaded and cased if the permit holder leaves the vehicle
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry prohibited if the premises are posted; otherwise permitted per Attorney General opinion
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry prohibited in passenger terminals; parking facilities permitted unless posted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry prohibited in buildings; carry permitted on outdoor grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Virginia requires a license to carry a concealed handgun. Permits are available for both residents (through local circuit courts) and non-residents (through State Police) and are valid for 5 years. Virginia recognizes permits from all other states, as long as the permittee is at least 21 years old, possesses a state-issued photo ID, and has not had a Virginia permit revoked.

Carry is lawful in most public areas but is prohibited in churches (if posted), courthouses, airports, private businesses that prohibit carry, executive and legislative branch offices with posted signage, the State Capitol, any Virginia-owned or leased building (including rest areas), K-12 schools and sponsored events, most colleges and universities, public buildings, parks, and parades where local ordinances prohibit carry. Polling places are off-limits during voting hours.

**Without a Permit**

A loaded handgun may be carried in a vehicle if it is in plain view or secured in a container or vehicle compartment — including the glove compartment, console box, trunk, or a briefcase. "Container carry" applies exclusively to handguns. Open carry on foot is permitted but may be subject to local ordinance restrictions.

The state prohibits non-permit holders from carrying loaded center-fire firearms with capacities greater than 20 rounds in many urban areas.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Localities have the authority to prohibit carry in parks and buildings they control, as well as at permitted outdoor events like parades or demonstrations. Postings must be in place for prohibitions to be enforceable.

Long guns in vehicles are allowed if cased and stored in the trunk or rear storage area. Long guns must remain unloaded on Department of Natural Resources property (conservation and wildlife management areas) and National Forest lands. Some counties may require long guns to be unloaded unless possessed for "personal safety" reasons related to employment.

> ***Key Takeaway: Virginia is a split-personality state for armed travelers. Universal permit recognition and lawful open carry provide a strong foundation, but the absence of preemption means local jurisdictions — particularly Northern Virginia, Richmond, and other urban areas — may impose their own carry restrictions on parks, buildings, and events. The 20-round magazine capacity restriction for non-permittees in certain urban areas is an unusual provision. Vehicle carry of loaded handguns is permissive (plain view or compartment), but the rest area restriction (buildings off-limits, outdoor grounds permitted) is a notable quirk. Always check local ordinances before carrying in Virginia's urban areas.***
