**Chapter 57**

**Washington**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 30/100 — A state trending sharply toward restriction. Limited out-of-state permit recognition, assault weapons and magazine bans, strict vehicle carry rules for non-permittees, and expanding location-based prohibitions.*

Washington's expansive wilderness has long fostered a gun ownership culture, but recent political changes are bringing increasingly tight regulations. The state bans the sale and importation of magazines holding more than 10 rounds and assault weapons, does not offer permitless carry, recognizes only a narrow set of out-of-state permits, and imposes strict vehicle carry rules on non-permittees. Open carry restrictions at demonstrations, the State Capitol, polling places, and government meetings add further complexity.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for possession; sales require proof of training
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** "Streetsweeper"-style shotguns prohibited; sale and importation of magazines exceeding 10 rounds and assault weapons prohibited
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance; bump stocks prohibited
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption statute in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** No enhanced Castle Doctrine; stand-your-ground not codified in statute
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas for permit holders and those 21 and older; prohibited at demonstrations, State Capitol, polling places, and government meetings for everyone
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents and non-residents; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** No
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional and narrow — the other state must recognize Washington permits, not issue to persons under 21, and require fingerprint-based background checks
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Handguns must be unloaded and secured in commercial gun cases; long guns must be unloaded
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Prohibited through administrative code
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Washington permit holders may carry loaded handguns while picking up or dropping off a student
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Permit holders may carry while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities unless posted
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Carrying a loaded handgun — either concealed on one's person or in a vehicle or on public transportation — requires a valid license. Residents apply through their local police or sheriff's department, while non-residents can apply in person at any police or sheriff's office. Licenses are valid for five years.

Washington recognizes out-of-state permits only from jurisdictions that also recognize Washington permits, conduct fingerprint-based background checks, and do not issue permits to individuals under 21. Non-residents with recognized permits may carry handguns only and must ensure the weapon is locked and hidden from view if left in a vehicle.

Carry is prohibited in courtrooms, police stations, bars, ballot-counting centers, administrative hearing centers, K-12 schools, daycares, mental health facilities, horse racetracks, outdoor music festivals, and many colleges.

**Without a Permit**

Handguns transported in a vehicle must be unloaded and secured in a proper gun case. Loaded magazines may be stored in the vehicle but cannot be inserted into a handgun. On foot, individuals without a permit may carry a loaded, concealed handgun only while engaged in activities such as hunting, fishing, camping, or target shooting.

Open carry is generally permitted in most public areas. However, beyond standard prohibited zones, carrying firearms is also illegal in zoos, aquariums, libraries, and transit stations (including bus stops). Cities may impose further restrictions in stadiums and convention centers, and firearms are prohibited at school board meetings. All local restrictions must be clearly posted.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Unloaded rifles and shotguns may be stored in gun racks or cases within a vehicle. A long gun is only considered "loaded" if a round is in the chamber or a loaded magazine is attached. Loaded magazines nearby are permitted as long as they are not inserted. All firearms must be unloaded when carried in snowmobiles.

Open carry of any firearm is prohibited at demonstrations, on the grounds of the State Capitol, at polling places, and during meetings of local governing bodies. This restriction applies to everyone, including permit holders. However, concealed carry with a valid permit is allowed in these locations.

The sale and importation of magazines holding more than 10 rounds, as well as assault weapons, is prohibited.

> ***Key Takeaway: Washington has become an increasingly challenging state for armed travelers. The narrow reciprocity requirements mean most out-of-state permits are not recognized — verify yours before traveling. The assault weapons and magazine capacity bans prohibit bringing standard-capacity magazines or restricted firearms into the state. Non-permittees face strict vehicle carry rules (unloaded, cased handguns only). If you plan to carry in Washington, obtaining a Washington non-resident permit is strongly recommended. Pay close attention to the expanding list of prohibited locations, including transit stations, libraries, zoos, and aquariums.***
