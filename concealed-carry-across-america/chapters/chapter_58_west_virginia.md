**Chapter 58**

**West Virginia**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 82/100 — Permitless carry at 21, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, broad vehicle carry rights (including loaded long guns), and parking lot protections. A state that honors its Civil War-era firearms heritage.*

West Virginia's spirit was on full display during the Civil War, when many of her native sons brought their privately owned firearms to the fight — cementing gun ownership as a core part of the state's heritage. In 2016, honoring that legacy, the state adopted permitless carry for law-abiding individuals aged 21 and older. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, and recently expanded vehicle carry rights that now permit loaded long guns, West Virginia is a solidly gun-friendly destination.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect, but some restrictions in city-owned buildings and recreation centers
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas, but some localities may regulate independently
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Standard permits issued to residents and non-residents (21+); provisional permits for ages 18-20; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any U.S. citizen or legal resident 21 or older; military personnel under 21 may carry concealed handguns
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Conditional — maintains reciprocity agreements with other states; out-of-state permittee must be a non-resident and at least 21
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns only (permitless carry extends to any lawful deadly weapon)
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded handguns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle by persons 21 and older; loaded long guns may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Permit holders may carry loaded, concealed handguns
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** West Virginia permit holders may carry loaded handguns; must be at least 21 and lock the firearm in a secure compartment when the car is unoccupied
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may not prohibit firearms in vehicles
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by recognized licensees permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Sheriffs continue to issue concealed handgun permits to residents aged 18 and older. Provisional licenses are available for those between 18 and 20 and remain valid until the licensee turns 21. Standard licenses for individuals 21 and older are valid for five years and are also available to non-residents. These licenses apply only to handguns.

West Virginia maintains reciprocity agreements with other states, allowing permit recognition provided the out-of-state permittee is a non-resident and at least 21 years old.

Licensed individuals may carry loaded, concealed handguns in vehicles or on foot throughout most public areas. Some local ordinances may restrict carry in municipal buildings. Permit holders are also allowed to carry concealed, loaded handguns in most buildings and outdoor areas of state college campuses.

**Without a Permit**

Any U.S. citizen or legal resident aged 21 or older who is not a prohibited person may carry a concealed weapon — on their person or in a vehicle — in most public areas without a permit. This permitless carry law applies to any lawful deadly weapon, not just handguns.

Military personnel under 21 may carry concealed handguns without a license, but this does not extend to other types of weapons.

Open carry on foot is permitted in most public areas not restricted by local ordinance. Municipalities may limit open carry on city property through posted signage. However, storing a weapon in a parking lot is allowed as long as it remains concealed from view.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Loaded shotguns and rifles may be transported anywhere within a vehicle — the state recently eliminated the requirement that long guns be unloaded and cased. However, long guns must still be cased if carried on foot or transported within facilities and designated trails of state parks and forests.

Carry is prohibited for everyone in courthouses, schools (including vocational schools — though private K-12 institutions may set their own rules), the State Capitol, and any private or municipal property that posts signs banning firearms. Permit holders may keep loaded handguns in their vehicles at the State Capitol and on school grounds.

Most property owners cannot ban firearms stored in locked vehicles, provided the weapon is hidden from view.

> ***Key Takeaway: West Virginia is a strong state for armed travelers. Permitless carry at 21 extends to any lawful deadly weapon (not just handguns), vehicle carry of loaded long guns is now unrestricted, parking lot protections prevent employers from banning firearms in vehicles, and the Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground provides robust self-defense rights. The provisional permit for ages 18-20 and the standard permit remain valuable for interstate reciprocity and for access to college campuses. The main restrictions to watch are locally posted municipal buildings and the standard prohibited locations.***
