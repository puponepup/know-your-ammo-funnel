**Chapter 60**

**Wyoming**

*Firearm Friendliness Rating: 90/100 — The "Cowboy State" lives up to its name. Permitless carry, strong Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground, universal permit recognition, broad vehicle carry, and a deeply rooted firearms culture make Wyoming one of the best states in the country for armed travelers.*

Wyoming's rugged frontier heritage is reflected in its firearms laws. The state adopted permitless carry and continues to maintain one of the most permissive legal environments for gun owners in the nation. Combined with a Castle Doctrine, stand-your-ground protections, universal recognition of all out-of-state permits, statewide preemption, and a culture where open carry is commonplace and unremarkable, Wyoming is a top-tier destination for armed travelers.

**Quick-Reference Checklist**

- **Standard firearms & ammunition:** No license required for ownership or purchase
- **Semi-automatic firearms & standard-capacity magazines:** No restrictions on possession or sale
- **NFA items (machine guns, suppressors):** Lawful with proper federal compliance
- **Firearm law uniformity:** Preemption law in effect; firearms laws uniform throughout the state
- **Self-defense rights:** Enhanced Castle Doctrine enacted; stand-your-ground protections apply in public spaces
- **Open carry:** Lawful in most public areas and commonly accepted throughout the state
- **Concealed carry licensing:** Permits issued to residents only; five-year terms
- **Permitless carry:** Yes — any individual 21 or older (or 18 or older for residents) who may lawfully possess a firearm
- **Out-of-state permit recognition:** Automatic — recognizes carry permits from all other states
- **Weapons allowed under permit:** Handguns and other deadly weapons
- **Vehicle carry (non-permittees):** Loaded firearms may be carried anywhere in a vehicle
- **Vehicle carry at colleges:** Subject to college administrative policy
- **Vehicle carry at K-12 schools:** Wyoming permit holders may carry loaded handguns in their vehicles
- **Duty to notify law enforcement:** Required only upon direct request by an officer
- **Boondocking/RV carry:** Property owners may restrict firearms in vehicles on their premises
- **State parks:** Concealed handgun carry by licensees and permitless carriers permitted
- **Restaurants serving alcohol:** Carry permitted while dining in food-service areas
- **Houses of worship:** Carry permitted unless the facility has posted a prohibition
- **Hotels:** Subject to individual hotel policy
- **Airports:** Carry permitted in non-secure terminal areas and parking facilities
- **Highway rest areas:** Carry permitted in buildings and on surrounding grounds

**For Permit Holders**

Wyoming issues concealed firearm permits to residents who are at least 21 years old. Permits are valid for five years and authorize the carry of handguns and other deadly weapons. The state recognizes all out-of-state carry permits.

Permit holders may carry in most public areas with standard location-based restrictions applying to courthouses, government buildings with security screening, K-12 schools, and other posted locations.

**Without a Permit**

Wyoming's permitless carry law allows any individual 21 or older (or 18 or older for state residents) who may lawfully possess a firearm to carry concealed without a permit. Open carry is broadly permitted and culturally accepted throughout the state.

Loaded firearms — including handguns, rifles, and shotguns — may be carried anywhere in a vehicle without restriction.

**Restrictions That Apply to Everyone**

Standard location-based restrictions apply to courthouses, law enforcement facilities, K-12 schools, and government buildings with posted signage or security screening. Wyoming's preemption law ensures that local governments cannot enact their own firearms regulations, providing consistent laws statewide.

> ***Key Takeaway: Wyoming is one of the absolute best states in the country for armed travelers. Permitless carry, universal permit recognition, unrestricted vehicle carry of all firearm types, broad open carry acceptance, strong preemption, and a Castle Doctrine with stand-your-ground create a near-ideal legal environment. The prohibited location list is minimal and straightforward. The resident permit remains worth obtaining for interstate reciprocity when traveling to states that do not offer permitless carry. If you carry responsibly and observe the basic location restrictions, Wyoming is about as friendly as it gets.***
