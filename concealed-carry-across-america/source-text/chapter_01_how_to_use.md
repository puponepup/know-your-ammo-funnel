How to Use this Guide
Traveling with a firearm can be legally perilous, especially when crossing into states with vastly different laws. Many travelers have found themselves entangled in serious legal trouble—sometimes facing felony charges—for actions that would barely amount to a minor infraction in their home state.

Consider the real-life case of a North Carolina businessman on a road trip to Maine. While passing through New Jersey on the turnpike, he was stopped by a state trooper for speeding. During the routine stop, the officer asked if he had any firearms in the vehicle. Confident that he was compliant with the law, the traveler disclosed that he had a Glock 19 in his briefcase. He held a valid concealed carry permit from North Carolina and even offered to let the officer inspect the firearm.

What happened next was a shocking wake-up call. The trooper immediately drew his sidearm, pointed it at the driver, and ordered him out of the vehicle with his hands raised. The businessman—who had never been in legal trouble before—complied, only to be forced to the ground while backup was called in. He was arrested on the spot.

Despite his clean record, the man was charged with a felony and spent three days in a Newark jail. Although the charges were eventually reduced and he was placed in a diversion program, the incident could have resulted in prison time had he not had such an impeccable history.

His mistake? Assuming that his North Carolina permit protected him in New Jersey—a state with some of the strictest firearm laws in the country. Unfortunately, ignorance of local law is not a defense.

This story underscores why understanding each state's firearm laws is absolutely critical. What's legal in one state may be a serious offense just miles away. This guide exists to help you avoid such costly and traumatic mistakes.

This guide helps travelers avoid legal trouble when carrying firearms across state lines by outlining key laws and potential pitfalls. Each state, from Alabama to Wyoming, is given a one-page summary of the most relevant firearm regulations, with additional coverage for the District of Columbia, Canada, and Mexico.

A bar graph at the top of each page rates the state's firearm friendliness. Changes from the previous year are marked with a (+) or (–) and explained briefly; if unchanged, a summary of the state's rating is provided.

Vehicle carry, concealed carry, and general possession laws are presented in a clear, user-friendly format, making this guide an essential tool for safe and legal travel with firearms.
