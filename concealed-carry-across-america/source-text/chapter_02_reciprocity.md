# SOURCE: Reciprocity and Recognition (Page 4)

Many states recognize out-of-state concealed carry permits under specific conditions. Some offer automatic recognition of any valid permit, while others require reciprocal recognition or similar issuance standards. In those cases, recognition may be at the discretion of the state attorney general or police, making it subject to change or inconsistent enforcement. Travelers should always verify the current status of their permit before relying on it in another state, as reciprocity agreements can shift without notice.

A growing concern involves non-resident permits. While many states now issue permits to non-residents—and some make exceptions for military personnel—others have started refusing to recognize permits issued to individuals who don't reside in the issuing state. This can create legal uncertainty, particularly for those from restrictive states seeking alternatives.

When checking reciprocity, travelers should pay attention to whether the state recognizes non-resident permits and should note any states that refuse recognition to their own residents' out-of-state permits—these are underlined in the guide.

Additionally, most states limit permits to handguns; only some allow other weapons, as noted in the Traveler's Checklist. Travelers should always carry their permits on them and be alert to local restrictions, including prohibited areas like government buildings and parks.
