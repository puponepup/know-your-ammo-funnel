# SOURCE: Right of Self-Defense (Page 5)

The Right of Self-Defense section in the Traveler's Checklist provides an overview of how each state treats a citizen's right to defend themselves. It notes whether a state has adopted the NRA-model Castle Doctrine within the last 19 years and whether it recognizes a "stand your ground" policy in public spaces—allowing individuals to meet deadly threats with force without a duty to retreat.

While nearly all states recognize the common law Castle Doctrine (the right to defend oneself at home), many expanded versions also apply to places outside the home, such as vehicles or any location where a person has a legal right to be. Some states rely on case law rather than statute for these protections—these are noted as "not codified."

This section is not intended to be a detailed legal analysis, but rather a practical reference point to help travelers quickly gauge a state's general stance on self-defense rights. In today's legal climate, citizens may hesitate to defend themselves due to fear of prosecution or civil suits—often brought by politically motivated prosecutors or even by their attackers. This guide helps identify which states support lawful self-defense and which ones are more likely to penalize it.
