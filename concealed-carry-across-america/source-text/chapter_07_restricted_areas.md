# SOURCE: Universally Restricted Areas (Page 8)

Firearm carry is universally prohibited in certain locations, even for individuals who hold a concealed carry permit. Federal facilities such as post offices—including their parking lots—courthouses, administrative offices, and certain federally managed lands, such as properties overseen by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, strictly forbid firearms inside buildings and often on the surrounding grounds as well. The Corps enforces a comprehensive "no firearms" policy on any land it owns, manages, or leases, including recreational areas and campgrounds. This can lead to confusion, especially around lakes formed by Corps dam projects, where shoreline property may be federally owned, and sometimes intermingle with state park boundaries. Travelers should be aware of these boundary lines and follow McClure-Volkmer transport rules when crossing into Corps-managed territory.

Military installations also prohibit visitors from carrying firearms and place significant restrictions on possessing firearms within vehicles. A recent Department of Defense directive (5210.56) clarifies policies regarding gun possession on military property and outlines limited circumstances in which qualified personnel may be authorized to carry personal firearms on base. All visitors—military or civilian—should confirm policies in advance before bringing firearms onto military grounds.

At the state level, most jurisdictions prohibit firearm carry inside preschool, primary, and secondary (K–12) school buildings. Violations often carry severe penalties. In addition, federal law bars the possession of firearms within 1,000 feet of any K–12 school property. However, there are exceptions: individuals with a concealed carry permit issued by the state where the school is located, persons transporting unloaded firearms in locked containers within their vehicles, and private property owners within the zone are exempt from this federal restriction. Those carrying a loaded firearm without a valid license within that 1,000-foot boundary may be in violation of federal law, though this provision is typically enforced as an additional charge in drug or gang-related cases.

Firearms are also commonly prohibited in other sensitive locations, including law enforcement offices, jails and detention centers, courthouses, polling places, most legislative assemblies, government buildings where official business occurs, as well as in mental health facilities and day care centers. The Guide provides state-specific information regarding these locations.

Additionally, restrictions may apply to businesses that primarily serve alcohol, such as bars. Regulations vary by state—some prohibit firearms entirely, while others allow carry so long as no alcohol is consumed. Because these laws differ widely, travelers are advised to avoid carrying in such establishments unless they are fully aware of the applicable rules in that state.
