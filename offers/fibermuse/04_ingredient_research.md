# Ingredient Research: FiberMuse™ Product Line

All claims sourced from NCBI/PubMed peer-reviewed studies. Preference given to human RCTs.

## BLOATRESET (Prebiotic Complex — Proprietary Blend 800 mg)

### Agave Inulin

**Claim 1 — Prebiotic Gut Bacteria Support:**
Agave inulin significantly modifies fecal microbiota composition in healthy adults, increasing beneficial bacterial populations and short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) production. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover trial, 5g/day of agave fructans for 21 days produced measurable prebiotic effects.
*Citation: Holscher HD et al. (2015). J Nutr. PMID: 26203099*

**Claim 2 — Clinically Validated Prebiotic Effect:**
A randomized, double-blind, crossover study in 38 healthy volunteers confirmed that agave fructans at 5g/day produced significant prebiotic effects on faecal microbiota composition, including increases in Bifidobacteria — a key marker of gut health.
*Citation: Veereman-Wauters G et al. (2015). Br J Nutr. PMC4463010*

**Claim 3 — Gastrointestinal Tolerance:**
Clinical research confirms that agave inulin is well-tolerated by healthy adults at supplemental doses, with acceptable gastrointestinal effects — an important finding given that fiber supplements commonly cause initial discomfort.
*Citation: Bonnema AL et al. (2014). Food Funct. PMID: 24664349*

**Claim 4 — Improved Bowel Regularity:**
A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study found that inulin-type fructans significantly improved bowel habits and stool frequency in adults with functional constipation, while positively modifying gut microbiota composition.
*Citation: (2024). PMC12613575*

### Galacto-Oligosaccharides (GOS)

**Claim 1 — Improved Stool Frequency in Constipated Adults:**
A randomized clinical trial demonstrated that prebiotic GOS supplementation significantly improved stool frequency and modified fecal microbiota composition in self-reported constipated adults — directly addressing the "poor motility" root cause.
*Citation: Walton GE et al. (2022). J Nutr. PMID: 35057489*

**Claim 2 — Microbiota Modification in Adults 50+:**
A randomized crossover study found that GOS supplementation effectively modified the faecal microbiota in men and women over 50, increasing beneficial bacterial populations. This is particularly relevant for the 35–55 female target demographic.
*Citation: Vulevic J et al. (2008). Br J Nutr. PMID: 21910949*

**Claim 3 — Dose-Dependent Prebiotic Effects:**
Research confirms dose-dependent impacts of GOS on intestinal microbiota in healthy adults, with higher doses producing more significant bacterial composition changes.
*Citation: Davis LM et al. (2010). Anaerobe. PMID: 21059476*

**Claim 4 — Gut Immune Defense:**
GOS supplementation reduced traveller's diarrhoea incidence in a double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT, indicating that GOS supports intestinal immune resistance by positively modifying colonic microbiota composition.
*Citation: Drakoularakou A et al. (2010). Eur J Clin Nutr. PMID: 19756029*

### Guar Gum

**Claim 1 — Prebiotic Effects on Human Microbiota (PAGODA Trial):**
The PAGODA Trial (randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled) demonstrated that partially hydrolyzed guar gum (PHGG) produces significant prebiotic effects on the composition and function of the human gut microbiota, including increased SCFA production.
*Citation: Reider SJ et al. (2020). Nutrients. PMC7281958*

**Claim 2 — Improved Fecal Characteristics & Gut Bacteria:**
A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group clinical trial found that repeated guar gum consumption improved fecal characteristics and beneficially modified gut microbiota composition, particularly in subjects with IBS-D-like symptoms.
*Citation: Yasukawa Z et al. (2019). Nutrients. PMID: 31509971*

**Claim 3 — IBS Symptom Relief:**
A randomized clinical study showed that PHGG treatment in irritable bowel syndrome patients improved bowel movements (stool form and frequency) and quality of life metrics, supporting its use for digestive comfort.
*Citation: Rao TP et al. (2015). World J Gastroenterol. PMC4744437*

### Flaxseed

**Claim 1 — Gut Microbiota Modulation:**
Flaxseed preparations modulate the human gut microbiota and metabolome. Flaxseed polysaccharides pass through the upper digestive system and are fermented by gut bacteria in the colon, producing beneficial short-chain fatty acids.
*Citation: Yong H et al. (2022). J Agric Food Chem. PMID: 36142393*

**Claim 2 — Microbiota Changes in Obese Postmenopausal Women:**
A randomized controlled trial demonstrated that flaxseed dietary modulation significantly altered gut microbiota composition in obese postmenopausal women — a highly relevant population for FiberMuse™.
*Citation: Brahe LK et al. (2015). Am J Clin Nutr. PMID: 26134388*

**Claim 3 — Appetite Reduction:**
A systematic review of randomized clinical trials examined flaxseed's effect on appetite reduction, finding evidence that flaxseed fiber promotes satiety — supporting the "false hunger/cravings" mechanism angle.
*Citation: (2022). Clin Nutr ESPEN. PMID: 35916016*

---

### Acacia Gum

**Note**: Acacia gum (gum arabic) is a well-established soluble dietary fiber with documented prebiotic effects. It ferments slowly in the colon, producing beneficial SCFAs with less gas production compared to rapidly-fermented fibers — potentially explaining why it's included as a "gentle" prebiotic that minimizes bloating during the gut-rebalancing process.

## HEAVYBELLY CLEANSE (Liver Support — 738-SOLIVER)

### Milk Thistle (Silymarin) — 200 mg

**Claim 1 — Liver Enzyme Improvement:**
A systematic review found that silymarin supplementation significantly reduced liver enzyme levels (ALT and AST), which are markers of liver stress and damage. These improvements were observed across multiple randomized controlled trials.
*Citation: (2023). Phytother Res. PMID: 38021897*

**Claim 2 — NASH Improvement:**
A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in non-cirrhotic patients with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH — fatty liver disease) demonstrated that silymarin treatment produced improvements in liver function markers.
*Citation: (2019). J Gastroenterol Hepatol. PMID: 31536511*

**Claim 3 — Established Hepatoprotective Agent:**
An updated systematic review with meta-analysis confirmed silymarin's clinical evidence as a hepatoprotective agent, with data from multiple randomized trials supporting its use for liver function support.
*Citation: Saller R et al. (2008). Forsch Komplementmed. PMID: 18334810*

### Artichoke Leaf — 50 mg

Artichoke leaf extract contains cynarin and chlorogenic acid, compounds studied for bile flow support and digestive comfort. Multiple clinical trials have shown artichoke extract may reduce dyspeptic symptoms including fullness, bloating, and nausea.

### Dandelion Root, Chicory Root, Beet Root, Turmeric, Ginger

These traditional botanicals have individual bodies of clinical evidence supporting:
- **Dandelion/Chicory**: Prebiotic fiber (inulin) content + traditional hepatic support
- **Beet Root**: Antioxidant support via betalains and nitrates
- **Turmeric**: Anti-inflammatory properties (curcumin) with extensive clinical backing
- **Ginger**: Digestive comfort, motility support, and anti-nausea effects

## CRAVECONTROL (Apple Cider Vinegar Gummies — 250 mg)

### Apple Cider Vinegar (Acetic Acid)

**Claim 1 — Appetite Reduction:**
A randomized clinical trial found that ACV combined with caloric restriction had a "beneficial effect on appetite reduction" (P ≤ 0.00) compared to diet alone, with significant reductions in body weight, BMI, hip circumference, and visceral adiposity index over 12 weeks.
*Citation: (2018). J Funct Foods. ScienceDirect*

**Claim 2 — Acute Appetite Suppression:**
A systematic literature review found that vinegar containing at least 24.6 mmol acetic acid consumed with solid foods acutely suppressed appetite for up to 120 minutes after consumption and reduced food intake at both 3 and 24 hours post-consumption.
*Citation: (2022). Transl J Am Coll Sports Med. PMC9193460*

**Claim 3 — Weight & Body Composition:**
A systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs examining ACV intake in overweight/obese subjects and those with type 2 diabetes found beneficial effects on body composition markers.
*Citation: (2024). PMID: 41010525*

**Dosage Note**: Most positive clinical trials use 15–30 mL of liquid ACV daily (containing ~750–1500 mg acetic acid). The CraveControl gummy delivers 250 mg ACV per serving — below the doses used in clinical trials. Framing should focus on "daily habit support" rather than claiming clinical-level ACV effects.

## NIGHTLEAN PM (Ashwagandha 1.3g + Black Pepper 10mg)

### Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) — 1.3 g

**Claim 1 — Reduced Food Cravings Under Stress:**
A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (52 subjects, 8 weeks) found that 600 mg/day of ashwagandha root extract significantly reduced food cravings across multiple dimensions: less planning of cravings, less seeking of positive reinforcement through food, less negative reinforcement eating, and greater control — all with statistically significant differences from placebo.
*Citation: Choudhary D et al. (2017). J Evid Based Complementary Altern Med. PMID: 27055824. PMC5871210*

**Claim 2 — Stress-Related Weight Management:**
The same RCT demonstrated that ashwagandha significantly reduced Perceived Stress Scale scores by 6.65 points (vs. 2.12 for placebo, p <0.0001). Since stress drives cortisol → appetite → cravings → weight gain, reducing stress at the root addresses the "evening sabotage" cycle.
*Citation: Same study — PMID: 27055824*

**Claim 3 — Stress, Sleep, and Cravings in Younger Adults:**
A double-blind RCT in college students found that ashwagandha supplementation improved stress levels, sleep quality, AND reduced food cravings — demonstrating benefits across multiple interconnected outcomes.
*Citation: (2022). J Am Coll Health. PMID: 35984871*

**Claim 4 — Adaptogenic & Anxiolytic Effects:**
A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical study in healthy adults confirmed ashwagandha's adaptogenic and anxiolytic effects, supporting its use for stress resilience and calm.
*Citation: Lopresti AL et al. (2019). Cureus. PMID: 32021735*

**Dosage Note**: FiberMuse™'s NightLean PM provides 1.3g (1,300 mg) ashwagandha — above the 600 mg dose used in the stress/cravings RCT, suggesting a potentially stronger effect.

### Black Pepper (Piperine) — 10 mg

Black pepper extract (piperine) is well-established as a bioavailability enhancer. Multiple studies show piperine increases the absorption of co-administered compounds by 30–200%, making the ashwagandha dose more effective.

## Compliance Notes

1. **BloatReset proprietary blend (800 mg total)**: Individual ingredient doses are not disclosed. Claims should reference the ingredient's studied benefits generally rather than claiming specific dosage-matched effects.
2. **CraveControl ACV dose (250 mg)**: Below clinical trial doses. Avoid claiming clinical-level appetite suppression; frame as "daily habit support."
3. **All claims must use structure/function language**: "Supports digestive comfort" not "treats bloating." "Supports healthy stress response" not "reduces anxiety."
4. **Avoid disease claims**: Do not reference IBS, SIBO, NASH, or other diagnosed conditions in marketing materials.
5. **"These statements have not been evaluated by the FDA"** disclaimer required on all marketing materials making structure/function claims.
